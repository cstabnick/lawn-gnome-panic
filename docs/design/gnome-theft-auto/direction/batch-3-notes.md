# Gnome Theft Auto: direction notes for the 70-quest story (batch 3)

Extends [bible.md](bible.md) for the story rebuilt as 70 rows in four acts. Row numbers, ids, regions and cast come from
`docs/roadmaps/gta-batch-3/E04/spine.md`; the story outline and the character bible expand them. Section numbers here
are this file's own.

---

## 1. Purpose

The bible directs the 37 story scenes and the side scenes of the first game. This file carries the same directing into
the 70-quest story: a curve for four acts instead of five chapters, the running jokes moved to their new rows and
joined by new ones, the rules that make every quest readable on its own (context) and every act travel (world use),
a shot-variety pass, a time of day for every scene, and the sound identity of each new place.

Where this file and the bible overlap, **the bible rules**: the five laws, the pacing numbers (`PACING`), the camera
table, the hat ladder and the sincere-beat rule all stand. Nothing here changes a checker number. Old chapters map to
acts as: chapters 1 to 5 become acts I to IV plus the finale (the old chapter 4 and 5 are the act IV rows 54 to 70).

### Tone ladder per act

**Act I, funny first, sincere once.** Funny: the debt on credit, Hyssop's notes, the cat's cap, Reginald's calipers, the
night-watch, Cardoon's toolbox, the diner paying in a meal. Sincere: Pip on the Drainpipe Court bench (#14, the first
sincere beat of the game), the photograph of Reginald and Juniper as children (#12, a short sad-funny exchange). The
jokes stop on the bench at #14 and on the photograph; nothing comic is added to either button. The act ends on Vesper's
threat after the tribunal (#15), with the Green's ribbon as the last bright image, not the last image.

**Act II, the comedy sharpens, then the floor goes.** Funny: the barometers, the unlicensed weather, Sorrel's tapes, the
spades chasing a small mole, the Flock's sermon on one leg, Cam's night at the track. Sincere: the Tablet (#23, the
bible's first scene where nobody jokes), the Knot-Hole (#26, Pip does not pour the vial), Granny's crack and the silo
(#31), the party (#34, warm and a little too perfect). Rule: after #26 and #31 there is a laugh before the next sincere
scene; the drive back from the Knot-Hole is that laugh. From #35 the jokes stop for the run: the raid has one gag at
most and none on Pip's arm.

**Act III, jokes come from the strangers, not the crew.** Funny: polar bears, the Capwood Warden's fines, the Spokewheel,
Dame Peony's pro, a mole that moved a ball, the Flock stepping aside on one leg. Sincere: the couch in Saucer Court
(#39, warm), Driftwood at the sea (#43, the bible's castaway getting his sea), Wisteria's ledger (#50, quiet and
cold). The jokes stop for the Test Cut's third part (#52 p3): nothing works, the camera stays with the deck and does
not cut away to a reaction gag. Never two sincere scenes back to back; #38 to #39 is the model (the pond, then a gag
about a disguise).

**Act IV, the coalition is the joke and the heart.** Funny: the Mortar for a Mower, the mole and Diggs's salute, the Don
tapping four times, each cap on one table at All Hats, Chard realising he built for nobody (said flatly). Sincere:
Diggs's medal (#61), the Countess relenting (#65, brisk, not tender), Clara's photograph (#66, the bible's Clara
register: quiet, every sentence ends early), the rooftop at Saucer Court (#68), the finale's jam. The jokes stop for
Clara and for the jam completely; comedy right after either comes only from a character who is coping (Pip, Hyssop).

## 4. The context rule (L04)

Every quest, kept or new, is read against this checklist by whoever builds it. A quest that fails any line is not
done. The player may have skipped, rewound, or put the game down for a month: nothing can depend on a scene they
might not have seen.

- [ ] **Who is asking.** Comms step 0, or the first lines of the intro, say who is speaking (name, and their relation
  to someone the player knows if they are new), in their own voice and not as a read-out.
- [ ] **What they want.** One concrete thing, in the speaker's words ("my bikes back", "three barometers to three
  fields").
- [ ] **Where.** One place name, as the characters would say it. Never a coordinate, never a block number (bible
  section 8, fault 8).
- [ ] **Why now.** Something has just happened or is about to: a deadline, a threat, an arrival. A quest with no
  "now" is a briefing; add the thing that went wrong.
- [ ] **What the player gets.** Said or shown: acorns, a vehicle, a favour, a door opening. Cosmetic outcomes are
  named as such.
- [ ] **The journal says "Previously".** One line, written as the speaker's summary of the story so far, not a
  system note. Example: "Previously: Vesper has the Acorn plates. The crew is hiding up north."
- [ ] **No quest depends on a skipped scene.** If row N+1 refers to a person or a fact from row N, row N+1 says it
  again in a clause (not a recap). A fact planted in a scene is planted twice, once in the scene and once in the
  journal or the comms line.
- [ ] **A new character introduces themselves.** On first appearance they say their name and their relation to
  someone the player knows ("Hyssop Doormat. 3A. I knew the last tenant."). The subtitle card also carries the
  name tag.
- [ ] **A far place says how to get there.** Anywhere far from Turfton (Saltbucket Shore, Rootwell, Snowcap Rise,
  Capwood, Pinwheel Ridge, Rutbottom, Trellisdown, Wader's Rest, the Burrow Door, Hedgeworth) names how to get there
  in the briefing (the road out, the ferry, the ramp, the Kicked Board), and the waypoint is on the map before
  the scene ends.
- [ ] **A kept quest keeps its old lines** unless the spine says it changes. Rewrites add a clause, not a scene.
- [ ] **The choice quests say what is at stake** (#21, #62) and name the cosmetic or epilogue consequence in the
  journal after the fact.

## 6. Shot variety (feeds L73)

The user likes the intent and the angles of the directed shots and wants more variety, not a different style. Keep the
bible's grammar (section 5): coverage over stage spots, singles tighten as it escalates, lock off for jokes, never move
during a punchline. Vary the *choice among equals*. Twelve notes for the 70-quest scenes and for E09's pass over the
existing ones.

1. **A different establishing shot per region.** Not every arrival is a high slow dolly. Downtown: street-level dolly
   down the grid. Mossy Wood: a low shot looking up through trunks. The Fells and Snowcap Rise: a locked wide with
   a small figure in the lower third. The Flats: a long-lens compression of a heat-hazed horizon. Trellisdown: a
   rack of vine rows leading to the manor. Saltbucket: a slow pan along the waterline. Three scenes in a row in one
   region may reuse the type, but never the same framing.
2. **A signature shot per new area, once per quest.** Saucer Court: a straight-down shot of the courtyard saucer.
   The Underspan: a shot from the underside of the Unfinished Span, the Skyway as a roof. Hedgeworth: a ground-level
   shot through blades of grass. Loamdeep: a doorway framing, always lit from inside. Rootwell: a symmetrical quad
   with the library tower. Pinwheel Ridge: a low wide with pinwheel blades crossing the lens. Snowdrop Slope: a
   downhill follow cam. Capwood: a shot through two toadstool stalks. The shot returns in the area's later quests,
   shortened.
3. **Arrivals at far areas are shot in three beats.** The road (a moving wide, follow), the threshold (a locked
   shot of the gate, ramp, ferry or door), the first face (an MCU of the first local). The Kicked Board, the Burrow
   Door and Dean's Leap are thresholds, not scenery.
4. **Crane or drone-high wide: five times in 70 rows.** Only when the scale of a place is the story: the first view
   of Saltbucket Shore (#43), the Anthill from the quad (#46), Hedgeworth's mown scar (#52 final), the coalition
   camp at the overlook (#64 final), the Combine leaving the Compost Heap (#69). Everywhere else keep to human height.
5. **Moles at eye height.** Moles are smaller than gnomes. Shoot Nubbin and the Burrowfolk at their own eye line
   (camera at ground level, looking level), and shoot gnomes talking to them from below their usual angle, not
   looking down. Never look down on a mole in a two-shot. When Diggs salutes a mole (#60), the camera is on the mole's
   level and the salute comes from above, then holds.
6. **The Spokewheel's rotating view.** The Spokewheel is a ride (#43). Shoot it with a slow orbit that rotates with
   the gondola, so the horizon tilts and the sea comes round; keep the passengers in the centre square (the phone
   rule). No handheld on the ride itself; handheld only on the ground after.
7. **Interiors take tighter framing.** The Double Pot sales floor, Brassknock Manor, the Woolly Sock Lodge, the
   Clubhouse, the bank, flat 3B: use MS and MCU, a lower ceiling, a lamp or window as the light source. One wide to
   place the room, then stay in. Mole halls use no wide at all except for the first look.
8. **Ski, rally and race follow cams vary by leg.** Snowdrop Slope (#37): a downhill chase cam behind the player
   with a cut to a side track on the turn. The Capwood Run (#40): a low bumper cam in the toadstools, then a
   side-on at the fords. Rutbottom (#42): a high-banked pylon camera at the turns. Do not use the same follow
   height twice in one race scene. The rally and the dirt bowl get `race-intro` lengths (15-30 s).
9. **Alternate the confrontation pair.** The bible's low/high pair (strong party low, cornered high) is the default;
   in act III and IV alternate it with a level two-shot when both parties are equals (Dame Peony and the player,
   the Countess and Vesper). Equals share a frame; only Vesper never does until #50.
10. **Reaction shots have a rotation.** The bible says every big line gets one. Rotate whose face it lands on: the
    player (the audience), the one the line is about, a third party watching. Do not give the player the reaction
    more than twice in a scene.
11. **Cutaways to inserts, not to wides.** To break a long exchange use an insert (a ticket, a medal, a photograph,
    a ledger, a permit) of 1-2 s rather than a reverse. The Countess's ledger (#50), Diggs's medal (#61), Clara's
    photograph (#66) and Hogweed's permits are the inserts of the act.
12. **Vary the hold on a gag.** The bible gives 1.5-3 s after the gag lands. Alternate between the long wide
    hold (the Flock on one leg), a held reaction (Basil at the mole), and a button cut on the gag itself (a pie
    appearing). A scene with two gags uses two different holds.
13. **Night scenes get one practical light and one rim.** A lit window, headlights, lantern or a neon sign in frame
    (bible section 8, fault 15), plus a pale hat in the foreground. Vary the light's colour per place (sodium
    orange downtown, blue-white in the Fells, warm yellow in Loamdeep, cold pink at the Saltbucket sign) so night is
    not one look.
14. **Vesper's frame stays his.** Variety never reaches him: low, alone, still, push-in only, no handheld, never a
    shared frame until #50 (a single deliberate break, then back to alone). The same applies to the Combine.

## 7. Time of day per scene

scenes keep the time they have today (the engine's `sky: 'golden'` reads as late afternoon or dusk; scenes with no

change is covered by E06's time-lapse transition: a short cut to the sun or lamps moving, not a title card. Where two
consecutive scenes are at the same time, no transition is used. "(as built)" marks an existing time.

## 8. Sound notes

One sonic identity per new area, to feed E09's sound bible. Each is a bed plus one signature sound. All of them stay
inside the bible's section 6 rules (ambience changes on the cut to a new place, silence is a cue, at most two stingers
a scene).

- **Saltbucket Shore.** Surf and a low swell; gulls too small to see; the planks of Long Spoon Pier creaking as if
  the pier were one big spoon. Signature: the Spokewheel's slow creak. Music: no theme of its own; `turfton` under a
  wind filter.
- **Rootwell and the Anthill.** A quad's murmur and a bell; under it a low continuous scrape of ants. Signature: the
  ants' rent tapping on stone. The Observatory dome opens with one long mechanical groan.
- **Rutbottom Dirt Bowl.** Engine pops, grit hitting tin, a starter's whistle. Signature: a bell at the flag.
- **Snowcap Rise and Pail Pond.** Wind, a rope tow's clack, creak of ice, a very relaxed huff. `frost` at lower level
  than the Fells. Signature: ice singing under the player.
- **Capwood and Pinwheel Ridge.** Capwood: dripping, dull ground, soft spore puffs, no echo. Pinwheel Ridge: a tonal
  whirr of the blades, pitched by wind, a metal pulse when a pinwheel is freed.
- **Trellisdown.** Crickets, glass clinks, a distant string tune; a hush indoors. Signature: the clink of a
  grape-dew bottle being set down. `villain` is kept out of the vineyard except when Vesper is present.
- **Wader's Rest.** Reeds, loch water, a chant held on one low note by the whole camp. Signature: one foot lifted and
  put down, a soft slap.
- **Loamdeep.** Earth, dripping, a long warm reverb; DJ Moleman's bass heard through walls before he is seen.
  Signature: root-lamps tinkling. `fescue` as a quiet bed.
- **Hedgeworth.** Hedge clippers, sprinklers, a ball in a cup, a hushed crowd. Signature: the thwock of a tee shot
  from far away. The Combine's mown scar is a long dry cut sound, once.
- **The Underspan, Even-Estates and the Double Pot.** The Underspan: clanks, whistles, the Dewdrop's squeak as it
  rolls. Even-Estates: a muffled hum, no birds, no wind, beige silence. The Double Pot: a lift tone and nothing else.
  Vesper's world is the one place the music drops out entirely.

## 9. Mobile

No story beat needs a desktop-only key. Every beat in the 70 rows plays by touch: the dialogue choices (#21, #62), the
`probe` and `visit` steps (standing on one leg at the sermon, enrolling at Rootwell), the new kinds (`golf-hole`,
`jam-game`, `skate-line`, `ski-run`, `tow-ski`, `ride-passenger`, `dig`, `interior-visit`, `roof-land`) when their
owner epics land, and the fallbacks named in the spine (`checkpoints` for the skate line and the ski run). Cutscene
framing follows the phone rule (bible section 1): speakers in the centre square, faces in the upper half, nothing that
matters in the bottom third while a line is up; the new area signature shots in section 6 keep their subjects inside
the centre square too. The context rule's comms lines are short enough to read at a phone width, and no quest relies
on hover or on a keyboard prompt in its briefing.
