## 1. Purpose in the story

The first sincere beat of the game. Pip says why he left Fescue ("I wanted to build. Not be built.") and nobody answers
him. The bible's sincere-scene rules apply: no gag, no music sting, one held silence. Cardoon's concession is warm and
dry; it plants that he kept the box dusted for Pip. Nothing about Pip's vial of Fescue soil is touched (a loose thread in
his lore entry), and Wendel is not named.

## 2. The player's feeling

- **In:** having won a game for a friend.
- **Out:** that Pip has told them something true, and that they are on his bench.

## 3. Characters: objective, obstacle, tactic

| Character | Objective | Obstacle | Tactic(s) | Change by the end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cardoon | to hand the box back with his dignity | his reputation | a conceding line, kept short | leaves, "Chief." |
| Pip | to say it once | his habit of talking fast | slow, plain sentences | has said it |
| The player | to be there | nothing to say | silence | stays on the bench |

## 4. Subtext

Pip's fear (the family embarrassment) is under every sentence; "Mine had my name on it" is the ledger of a life planned
for him.
