## 1. Purpose in the story

A parachute landing on a moving beige deck (outline). The player is on the machine; the machine knows.

## 2. The player's feeling

- **In:** they just bailed out of a plane over a street-long harvester.
- **Out:** on it. It is long. Something on the roof has noticed.

## 3. Characters

Pip, on the verge, has the one line (and the one gag). The HOA address system is a voice, not a character: Vesper's
staff, not Vesper (his PA unravels in part 3).

## 4. Subtext

Pip has waited the whole part for the player to land; "Legitimate!" is relief.

## 5. Beat sheet

| # | Time | Beat | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0-2.4 | Hook | Straight up from the road ahead of the machine: a canopy opens against the sky. |
| 2 | 2.4-4 | Setup | Closer: the canopy over the open deck. |
| 3 | 4-8 | Turn | The player's eyes: the beige deck comes up fast; a thud; the silk settles across it. |
| 4 | 8-11 | | Pip on the verge: "Touchdown! On a moving building! Legitimate!" |
| 5 | 11-18 | Button | Along the deck to the back of the bridge; up at the roof horns: "Visitor on deck. Porcelain Guard to the rear, please." `danger`. |

## 6. Shot list

The `sky-low` spot; a cut off the flank onto the canopy; a linear dolly down to deck eye height (POV) with the thud on
its end; Pip MCU at eye height; a slow POV dolly along the deck; a POV cut up to the horns. The landing is a point of
view on purpose: the deck is a cutscene prop, and the engine cannot stand an actor on a prop (the checker reads only
world colliders as floor), so the player is seen as their own eyes. If a future engine change lets actors stand on
props, a reverse MS of the player on the deck could go between beats 3 and 4.

## 7. Dialogue pass

- Pip: "Touchdown! On a moving building! Legitimate!" (his act I tic, said straight)
- HOA Address System: "Visitor on deck. Porcelain Guard to the rear, please."

## 8. Sound

Bed `wind`, music `chase` out before the button. Engine, canopy flutter twice, a whoosh and a thud, the silk, the
engine again, radio static from the horns; one stinger (`danger`).
