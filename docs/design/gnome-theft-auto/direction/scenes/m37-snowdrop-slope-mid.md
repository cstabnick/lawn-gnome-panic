## 1. Purpose in the story
The HOA's plows crest the rise behind the crew: the threat is real and has a shape. It sets up why the player skis the Wool Run (gates stop wide plows). Environmental story (L95): plows on a ridge, salting a hill that has never needed salt.

## 2. The player's feeling
- **In:** arrived at the foot of a ski slope after a long drive, a little pleased.
- **Out:** the clock is running; skis, tow, gates.

## 3. Characters: objective, obstacle, tactic

| Character | Objective | Obstacle | Tactic(s) | Change by the end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pip | get the player moving | the player asks why gates | fast plan, wrong science that happens to be right | shifts from fear to a plan |
| Plow driver | stop the sledding | none | an official voice and a permit | holds, blade down |
| Player | understand | none | one flat question | agrees |

## 4. Subtext
The HOA reads a slope as paperwork. Pip is scared and covers it with a plan.

## 6. Shot list
Pylon two-shot; Pip mcu; the rise (long, the plows driving in); the driver ms from below; Pip's flinch; Pip and player mcu coverage; the wide with both plows idling.

## 7. Dialogue pass
Six lines. The driver's "This slope has no salt permit. Please hold still." is the one joke the HOA gets.

## 8. Music and sound
`tension` under `snow`; tap (reels), engine-rev, a `danger` stinger on the reveal, the plow horn twice.

## 9. Blocking and vehicles
Two `hoa-van` plows (cutscene only; plow shape is the van with its blade) arrive along the rise from the south-east and stop 35 m out; headlights on at noon so they read as plows on snow. The player and Pip stay at the tow base.

## 10. The button
Plow horn, then the tow clacking on as though nothing had happened. The hill does not care.
