# Gnome Theft Auto: the 70-quest story outline

The written source of truth for the batch-3 main story (L84): four acts, 70 numbered quests (the 17 existing quests kept,
renumbered or rewritten in place; 53 new), six extended quests at the pivotal moments, and achievement candidates. Build
epics: E20 (rows 1-35), E21 (36-53), E22 (54-70). Names, cast ids, homes and world-change ids come from the E04 spine
([docs/roadmaps/gta-batch-3/E04/spine.md](../../roadmaps/gta-batch-3/E04/spine.md)); lore rulings R-158..R-167.

## The story in one paragraph

**Will Turfton stay crooked, loud and alive, or be mown even?** Vesper Thorne, porcelain chair of the HOA, is building
the Grand Combine, a city-sized harvester patterned on Fescue's Mower, to mow every yard to one beige height; its
ignition core is the Golden Acorn. The Combine is too big to build in one place, so it is built in four parts at the
edges of the map: the cutting deck (tested on Hedgeworth Links), the guidance or "Even Line" (computed at the Anthill
Observatory, Rootwell), the power (taken from Pinwheel Ridge) and the money (Countess Wisteria Brassknock of
Trellisdown). Act III is the player finding each part and a friend in each new place. The Flock of the One Leg know the
date of the Even Cut; the moles of Loamdeep, whom every gnome misreads, dig the crew into Fort Sandpit; and in the end
the Stopping Stone jams the machine and Vesper is stopped, not shattered.

## Rules for the build epics

- **Ids.** Kept quests keep their old id (saves and `QUEST_PLANS` depend on them). New quests: `m<NN>-<slug>`. Extended
  quests: the row's id is the **final part** (what a save records as finished); earlier parts are separate QuestDefs
  `<row id>-p1`, `-p2`, ... chained before it, so an old save that finished a kept extended quest stays finished.
- **Prereqs** chain row to row. Old saves: a kept quest already done stays done; inserted runs appear as catch-up quests
  because each run hangs off a quest the old save has. Nothing hides or resets. `ch5-even-cut` stays the last row.
- **Status:** `keep` (renumber, prereq may change), `rewrite` (same id; the row says what changes), `new`, `extended`.
- **Single-player.** Story quests stay single-player, as today: no `@lawn/mp` work. Every beat plays by touch.
- **Context (L04) and world use (L83):** see [direction/batch-3-notes.md](direction/batch-3-notes.md) sections 4 and 5. No
  more than two consecutive rows share a region tag; every new area has a main quest and a side quest.
- **Objective kinds** that do not exist yet are written `needs new kind: <name> (<epic>)`, each with a fallback in the
  row's notes. Unique objects are written `prefab needed: <name>`.
- **Side quests it opens** is filled from the schedule (the side quests whose unlock row is this row).

## Act 1: Fresh Glaze (rows 1-15)

The player arrives fired and owing: a Ride-On, a glowing crate and a Kiln-Debt. Act I answers one question, "Can the player pay their way out, and where do they live?" The answer is a loan, a flat at Saucer Court (3B, next to a neighbour who watches the stairs) and a string of errands for people who each want one small thing. Vesper Thorne starts as a rumour with a lien (a name on an eviction notice) and ends as the man who closes the act with a threat. Tension runs 3 (rows 1-2), 4 (3-5), 5 (6-8), 3 (9-11 light relief), 4 (12-13), 4 (14, the sincere beat), 6 (15). Comedy density is high through row 13; row 14 is the one sincere scene and gets no gag on top; row 15 closes on Vesper, never on a gag.
What the player wants: clear the debt, keep their head down. What they learn: the crew is the only home on offer, Vesper's money flows through the median leases, the Even-Estates and the Grand Combine, and every friendly face (Cam, Reginald, Pip) has something they are not saying.
Who changes: Pip goes from liability to someone who tells the truth about Fescue (row 14). Tansy goes from "not my problem" to a crew that takes sides in public (row 15). Cam is planted as the envious sweeper (rows 5, 8). Reginald shows a crack (row 12). Juniper goes from a client to an ally who rezones Vesper's blocks (row 15). Setups planted for later acts: the Dewdrop and the Whirligig citation (row 10), Granny Gnertrude's name (row 13), the Carburettors (row 5), the Mulch Club's war with the HOA (row 11), Hedda Galley and the Tattler (row 15), the four parts of the Combine are not named yet (the blueprint in row 8 shows one drawing, no parts list).
Regions visited: downtown, wilds (the first contract's tour), underspan, patio-heights, turfton-fringe, canal, south-highway.
Rows keep their old ids where kept; old saves that finished a kept quest stay finished, and rows 2, 5, 7, 9-14 appear as catch-up quests.

## Act 3: Cold Clay, Far Edges (rows 36-53)

The crew is on the run after the Salvage Raid: Side-Valve is seized, the Golden Acorn is gone, Pip's arm is cracked off
and Tansy has vanished. The question is whether a broken crew can find friends at the edges of the map before Vesper
finishes the Grand Combine. A radio fished out of Pail Pond (#38) shows the Combine is built in four parts: power
(Pinwheel Ridge), guidance (Rootwell), deck (Hedgeworth) and money (Trellisdown). The player wants the parts stopped and
learns that every edge has its own people: skiers, rally racers, a pirate, a professor, golfers, moles, a flock. Tension
runs 4 at the Fells, a warm 4 at the lodge and couch (#37-#39), climbs through the part hunts (5 at #41-#44, 6 at the
Anthill, 7 at Trellisdown) and peaks at 8 when the Combine cuts its test line and Vesper names the date of the Even
Cut (#52); #53 is the cold-air coda that points at act IV. Pip thaws toward the crew, Cam is off stage, Lulu and the
Carburettors get their own scenes, Peony and Basil go from Vesper's tenants to allies, and Vesper and the player share
one frame for the first time (#50). Regions: frostbite-fells, snowcap-rise, downtown, capwood, pinwheel-ridge, rutbottom,
saltbucket-shore, rootwell, hedgeworth, loamdeep, trellisdown, waders-rest. Sincere beats: #43 (Driftwood), #48
(Basil), #52 final. Laughs sit between them: #44, #45, #47, #51. The four parts, in the order the crew takes them: power
(#41), guidance (#46), money (#50), deck (#52, tested and not yet stoppable).

date of the Even Cut (the longest day) and the whole HOA behind it; the crew has a ruined bay, a stolen crate and a
list of friends made at the edges. The player wants to take Fort Sandpit's Whirligig and stop the Combine; what they
learn is that the plan works only if every outcast in Turfton sits at one table, and that the man in the throne is a
cracked gnome, not a monster. Curve: tension 6 at the Heist Board, climbing through the flares, the drop and the truce
to 8, a dip to 4 at the Diggs and Cam scenes, a triumph at the all-hats camp (7), a sincere low (3) at Clara's porch, a
spike to 9 when City Hall falls, a hush (3) on the night before, then 9, 10 at the Combine and release to 2 in the

stops saying "not my problem"; Diggs goes from hardliner to ally (salutes a mole); Cam is judged (free or leave); the

composure. Regions: dust-flats, underspan, pebblewick, downtown, wilds (canal, airfield, Rutbottom road, the overlook
camp), trellisdown, mossy-wood, turfton-fringe. Time of day runs dusk to dawn to the longest day. All rows single-player;
every beat plays by touch. Old-save note: `ch4-runway-flares`, `ch4-sandpit-drop`, `ch4-spade-truce` and
`ch5-even-cut` keep their ids and their done state; only their prereqs change.
