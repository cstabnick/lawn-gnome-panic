# Origin: why Gnomokemon, where from, why the grader stares, what the dog does

**Status: draft proposal.** Everything here is a first answer to four questions, written to be argued with. Lore
entries cite this file; when you change an answer, change it here and in the entries it names. Rules this obeys:
[Fescue's lore rules](../gnomomia/lore-rules.md) (history in threes, every creature wants something, no fourth
school of magic, leave threads loose) and the [core canon](../../lore/canon.md).

How to read each answer: **what gnomes say** (songs, records, memories that disagree), then **the truth** (for writers,
never shown to the player in one go), then **loose threads** (kept open on purpose).

## 0. Ground rules for Snap

1. **Snap is a Lab game, not a Gnomomia chapter.** It happens in a season of safaris with no date. It never shows the
   Mower move, the Heartspark break, the Stopping Stone fired or the Fence crossed (how the walk gets from the Lab
   into the walled garden is a loose thread). Gnomomia's twists stay Gnomomia's.
2. **Snap's Gnome Jesus and its dog are its own characters** (ruling R-030). They are not Gnome Company's Omnipresent
   Gnome Jesus or Giant Fetch Dog, which are apocrypha and never feed back into canon (R-005, R-012).
3. **Snap is a bridge game.** The Lab stands where Fescue meets the Yard's town (R-020). A ride can go into the
   garden (Fescue rules) or down the town road (the Yard's rules). Each region says which setting it follows.
4. **The camera sees; it never explains.** Lines in the game say what a creature wants. They never say what the
   Tall Ones did, where they went, or why.

## 1. Why are there Gnomokemon?

**The short answer:** the Tall Ones left things behind, and a lawn nobody tends keeps whatever it is given. A
Gnomokemon is a thing that wants something and, because nobody told it to stop, learned to walk. Read the "wants" in
the Gnomokedex and you are reading what each one was *for*: the Hoseleech wants water pressure, the Clockwork Frog
wants winding, the Bonsai Titan wants its pot, the Chimney Owl wants a chimney pot, the Kilnpup wants a hearthstone.

### What gnomes say

- **Planting (a rhyme, sung when skipping):** *"The Hand put the Garden down, and the Garden kept what fell. A
  thimble, a hose, a button, a bell. It asked each one, 'What are you for?' and the thing said what it wanted, and
  that was its name, and it walked."* The rhyme has no word for any of them. Children say "the Fell Things".
- **Charters (records):** the Crown's *Ledger of Beasts and Burdens* in the [Royal Archive](../../lore/entries/fescue/royal-archive.md)
  files them as "pests", priced by what they cost the Crown: rat tails, magpie bounties, beetle shell. The Ledger
  has no interest in *why* a magpie takes a Tall-eye. It only notes the loss.
- **Rumbling (memory):** gnomes who were there after the First Rumble say the pests "got stranger": things that
  glowed, things that rattled like iron, a mole king who would not stop walking. Around then a robed gnome at the edge
  of the lawn began drawing them instead of paying bounties on them. That is the Lab. The word "Gnomokemon" is his, and
  about thirty years old.

### The truth

- A "pest" and a "Gnomokemon" are **the same animals under two names.** Pest is what a gnome calls it when it is in
  his way. Gnomokemon is what the Lab calls it when there is a camera and no quarrel. Fescue's bestiary pests (stag
  beetle, hose leech, magpie thief, Clogmaw, Grubthorn) are Gnomokemon. Snap just spends more time asking what they want.
- **They come in three lineages** (the Gnomokedex's "family" column, tidied up):
  - **Grown:** a garden creature or plant the lawn has warmed for a long time (Mossback, Staghorn, Cactuskin, Kelpsprite).
  - **Dropped:** a Tall One thing that woke (Hoseleech, Clockwork Frog, Thimblekin, Rustcrab, Bonsai Titan).
  - **Shaped:** fired clay that was never a gnome: Kiln leavings, glaze scraps, unfinished pieces (Kilnpup, Unfired,
    Frosthound, Kilnwarden).
  - **The one exception** is Gnome Jesus (#036), who is a gnome. He is in the Gnomokedex because he wrote it.
- **The elements are not new magic.** Rule 7 allows three schools, and Snap keeps to them: **Bloom, Sun and Rain**
  are what the lawn gives. **Clay** and **Iron** are *materials*, not schools: Clay is the glaze that leaks from the
  [Kiln](../../lore/entries/fescue/the-kiln.md), Iron is the hum of the [Blade Shard](../../lore/entries/fescue/blade-shard.md)
  that wakes metal. That is why Iron Gnomokemon are rare and cluster near things the Mower touched.
- **Why photographs?** A specimen cannot be kept: you cannot glaze a mole. A Tall One kept the things it loved by
  framing them, and gnomes have never understood framing, but they understand attention. A Gnomokemon that has been seen
  *properly* settles into its pose. Being seen is the one thing a leftover can still be given. The Lab's grading
  (Size, Framing, Pose, the fifth thing) is a gnome's guess at what "properly" means.

### Loose threads (keep them loose)

- Whether the Gnomokemon are waiting for the Tall Ones, or have forgotten them. Both are in the Gnomokedex.
- What "kemon" means. Gnome Jesus gives three answers and they do not agree: "kin, mostly"; "kept, I think"; "it
  looked good on the sign".
- Whether the Gnomokemon have always been there, or are *more* since the Rumbling. The records say the first; the
  memories say the second. Nobody has counted.

## 2. Where do they come from?

**The short answer:** from the places a Tall One stopped. Each region's Gnomokemon are the things that were put down
there: the greenhouse's pots and toys, the shed's tools, the pond's pump, the hearth's stones. A region *is* the
spot where the lawn had the longest time to ask "what are you for?".

This is why Snap's regions are ordinary garden objects and not new lands, and why a new region should always start
from a question: **what did a Tall One stop doing here?** (The Greenhouse: tending. The Cistern: draining. The Dry
Yard: sitting on the porch.)

### Home and also-seen-in

The Gnomokedex lists one *home region* per species. A ride shows them somewhere else too. That is not a bug: a
Gnomokemon goes where its wanting goes. The rule the game should follow:

- **Home** is where the Gnomokedex page lives and where it is most common.
- **Also seen in** is anywhere its "wants" line is satisfied. A Kilnpup wants warm hearthstones, so it also appears
  on sun-baked Dry Yard rock and along the Stopping Road. A Hoseleech wants pressure and a sunny bank, so it is also
  in the Deeps and the Cistern. Write the reason next to every extra sighting.

The full table is in [regions.md](regions.md#home-and-also-seen-in).

One mismatch is a gift. **Grubthorn's home is Mossbottom Vale, but the Lawn ride meets him at the far north end.**
The ride goes south to north. Grubthorn is *leading his clan south, away from the shaking* (his entry in the
Gnomokedex, and the [mole clans](../../lore/entries/fescue/mole-clans.md)). He is mid-journey, going the other way
from the dog. Photograph him and you have photographed a king in flight.

## 3. Why is Gnome Jesus looking at me?

Gnome Jesus has two jobs in Snap. He **grades** every photo in the Grading Office, and he is also **Gnomokedex
#036**, a Mythic you can photograph at the end of the Starlight Greenhouse ride. Where the player meets his stare today: the
Grading Office (he sits behind the desk, turned to the player), the grading panel (a centred portrait facing the
camera) and the Lobby (beside Tag). In the Greenhouse he currently *orbits* and does not face the lens; the lore
says he stands in one spot every night and faces it (a follow-up, see below).

### What gnomes say

- **The street:** "His eyes follow you round a room." "He knows your score before you have framed the shot." "He says
  he is three thousand years old." "The halo is a Tall-eye." The first three are true enough to repeat.
- **What he says himself:** "I look at everyone. It is a courtesy, and also an audit."
- **The deed:** the Lab's lot is his, bought outright, in clay ([R-020](../../lore/rulings.md)). The Greenhouse
  is on it. He is standing in his own back garden.
- **Memory:** Mossbottom gnomes remember a bearded gnome in a white robe sketching in the vale the week after the
  First Rumble. He asked every slug what it wanted. He wrote the answers down.
- **His name:** it is a maker's mark, stamped on his foot like every gnome's. His reads JESUS. He did not choose it.
  He says the Kiln "was having a day". Fescue's gnomes call him Gnome Jesus because that is what the foot says. He
  does not preach, takes no tithes and is not a god. (That one is the Gnome Company parody, [R-005](../../lore/rulings.md).)

### The truth

He is looking at you for three reasons, and the third is the true one.

1. **You are holding the Eye-Box**, the Tall One's camera, which he has watched go out and come back with rolls of
   film for thirty years. The camera is the only thing in Fescue that makes a picture of a thing the way a Tall One
   did. To him, looking into it is looking at the Tall Ones' own opinion.
2. **Every page in the Gnomokedex has a photograph, except the last.** #036 is his. He has graded ten thousand
   photographs and nobody has ever taken an honest one of *him*.
3. **He is posing.** Straight at the lens, the same spot, every night, hoping the next photographer is the one. The
   surprise in his line ("Wait. That is me. That is ME.") is theatre. The *Pose* score rewards a subject facing the
   camera, and he knows it. He is afraid of Bronze.

This is why the game's grader is also the game's last creature: the Gnomokedex is not finished until someone frames
him with care and honesty, and he has built a whole Lab so that it can be.

### Design hooks this opens (not built)

- The Greenhouse has an **empty picture frame** propped on the potting bench, facing the door (see
  [regions.md](regions.md#6-starlight-greenhouse-the-glasshouse)). The player's best Gnome Jesus photo goes in it.
- A Bronze Jesus photo gets a line that is a little too quiet. A Divine one gets him to say nothing at all.
- He never mentions the frame.
- **Code follow-up:** make #036 hold still and face the camera when the camera is steady (today his spawn uses `orbit`,
  which turns him along his circle).

### Loose threads (keep them loose)

- **How old he is.** "Three thousand years of dust" and "even the Mower was jammed for three thousand years" are in
  his lines already. It is either bragging or literal. The Planting says there were **seven** [Kiln-born](../../lore/entries/fescue/kiln-born.md),
  that songs agree on only two, and that none are alive. Nobody says he is the eighth. Nobody says he is not.
- Who made him, and why the foot says what it says.
- Whether the halo is a Tall-eye. Nobody has asked to hold it.

## 4. What does the dog do?

The dog is **Tag**. He carries the photographer, the carrier gnome and the Eye-Box on his back along a fixed route
(the ride), and everything about how the ride works is Tag.

### What gnomes say

- **The name:** the dog wears a collar with a tag the size of a cart wheel, scratched with Tall One writing nobody can
  read. The gnomes call him "Tag", for the thing he wears. Nobody has asked what it is short for.
- **The walk:** he walks a round every day, from the Lab's door, past places the Tall Ones used to take him. The
  Lab's survey team worked out that if you sit on him, you are taken round his route. The ride's rail *is* his walk.
- **The saddle:** an old garden glove (gnomes say "an Empty Hand") stitched to a rope harness by the Lab's
  weavers. The photographer sits in the thumb-pocket.
- **In the Lobby:** he lies on the rug in front of the stairs and thumps his tail when the door opens, then puts his
  head down again. He faces the front door. Gnomes say "he is waiting for the walk". Some say he is waiting for someone.

### The truth

Dogs are the Tall Ones' pets ([R-012](../../lore/rulings.md): a dog is a trace of the Tall Ones, not a sighting of
one). Tag is Fescue's own dog, not the Yard's Biscuit and not Gnome Company's Fetch Dog; they only share a prefab
model. What he does in Snap, in order of importance:

1. **He carries the camera.** No gnome can walk the whole route and still frame a shot. The saddle is a camera
   platform, and the ride is a rail because a dog on a habitual route is predictable.
2. **He is the one thing every Gnomokemon tolerates.** Most creatures flee gnomes. They have known Tag all their
   lives. To a mole or a moth, Tag is weather. The photographer rides in his smell. That is why the "Tag nearby"
   response in [temperaments.md](temperaments.md) is the main thing that tells shy from skittish.
3. **His tail sets the beat.** When the Gnome Flute plays, Tag wags, and anything within a few dog-lengths falls into
   step and dances. (The game uses a 12-unit radius from the dog. That is the "wag".) The flute plays *Tag*, and the
   creatures follow him.
4. **He sets the pace.** The Speed control is a pat on the neck. Tag goes as fast as he wants and a pat only
   suggests. Skittish creatures like him slow.
5. **He rests.** Between rides he sleeps on the Lobby rug (R-019). He is never fed on screen. He never speaks. He
   is a very large, patient animal.

### Loose threads (keep them loose)

- Who Tag's person is, and whether they are coming back. He faces the door.
- What the tag says. Gnome Jesus has a theory. He does not share it.
- Why Tag's route passes through a Dry Yard that is not Fescue (see [regions.md](regions.md)): it is where he used to
  be walked, and the Tall Ones lived in more than one yard. Nothing more is said.

## 5. The Eye-Box (and the film)

Not asked, but it is the fifth thing in every answer above. The camera is the **Eye-Box**, a Tall One's camera found
in the Shed's crates. It takes a roll of 24 frames per ride. The film came from the same crate, and **nobody in Fescue
knows how to make more.** Jesus says the crate is deep and he has stopped counting. He counts at night.

This is the Snap-wide sad note: every ride uses up a roll of something that cannot be made again. The game never
says so. A line or two does.
