# Origin: why Gnomokemon, where from, why the grader stares, what the dog does

**Status: draft proposal.** Everything here is a first answer to four questions, written to be argued with. Lore
entries cite this file; when you change an answer, change it here and in the entries it names. Rules this obeys:
[Fescue's lore rules](../gnomomia/lore-rules.md) (history in threes, every creature wants something, no fourth
school of magic, one engine, leave threads loose) and the [core canon](../../lore/canon.md).

How to read each answer: **what gnomes say** (songs, records, memories that disagree), then **the truth** (for writers,
never shown to the player in one go), then **loose threads** (kept open on purpose).

## 0. Ground rules for Snap

1. **Snap is a Lab game, not a Gnomomia chapter.** It happens in a season of safaris with no date. It never shows the
   Mower move, the Heartspark break, the Stopping Stone fired, the Kiln lit, the Shed opened or the Fence crossed.
   Gnomomia's twists and quest items (Gnorwin's Ledger, the Great Valve, the valve seals) stay Gnomomia's.
2. **Snap's Gnome Jesus and its dog are its own characters** (ruling R-030). They are not Gnome Company's Omnipresent
   Gnome Jesus or Giant Fetch Dog, which are apocrypha and never feed back into canon (R-005, R-012).
3. **Snap is a bridge game.** The Lab stands where Fescue meets the Yard's town (R-020). A ride can go into the
   garden (Fescue rules) or down the town road (the Yard's rules). Each region says which setting it follows. The
   ride is a cut: it starts past the Lab's door and the film skips the way in. **How Tag gets from the town side
   into the walled garden is a loose thread. Nobody in the game says, and nobody asks.**
4. **The camera sees; it never explains.** Lines in the game say what a creature wants. They never say what the
   Tall Ones did, where they went, or why.
5. **Only one machine, one Tall One object understood.** Fescue has one engine (the Mower). In Snap, the Lab has
   worked out exactly one Tall One object, the Eye-Box, by thirty years of trial. Everything else is still renamed
   (R-030).

## 1. Why are there Gnomokemon?

**The short answer:** the Tall Ones left things behind, and a lawn nobody tends keeps whatever it is given. A
Gnomokemon is a thing that wants something and, because nobody told it to stop, learned to walk. Read the "wants" in
the Gnomokedex and, for a good many of them, you are reading what each was *for*: the Hoseleech wants water pressure,
the Clockwork Frog wants winding, the Bonsai Titan wants its pot. (Not all of them: a Grown creature like the Mossback
simply wants what a slug wants. The principle covers the Dropped; the rest are a lawn being kept company.)

### What gnomes say

- **Planting (a rhyme, sung when skipping):** *"The Hand put the Garden down, and the Garden kept what fell. A
  thimble, a hose, a button, a bell. It asked each one, 'What are you for?' and the thing said what it wanted, and
  that was its name, and it walked."* The rhyme has no word for any of them. Children say "the Fell Things".
- **Charters (records):** the Crown's *Ledger of Beasts and Burdens* in the [Royal Archive](../../lore/entries/fescue/royal-archive.md)
  files them as "pests", priced by what they cost the Crown: rat tails, magpie bounties, beetle shell. The Ledger
  has no interest in *why* a magpie takes a Tall-eye. It only notes the loss.
- **Rumbling (memory):** gnomes who were there after the First Rumble say the pests "got stranger": things that
  glowed, things that rattled like iron, a mole king who would not stop walking. Around then a robed gnome at the edge
  of the lawn began drawing them instead of paying bounties on them. That is the Lab. The word "Gnomokemon" is his, and
  about thirty years old.

### The truth

- A "pest" and a "Gnomokemon" are **the same animals under two names.** Pest is what a gnome calls it when it is in
  his way. Gnomokemon is what the Lab calls it when there is a camera and no quarrel. Fescue's bestiary pests (stag
  beetle, hose leech, magpie thief, Clogmaw, Grubthorn) are Gnomokemon. Snap just spends more time asking what they want.
- **They come in four lineages** (the Gnomokedex's "family" column, tidied up):
  - **Grown:** a garden creature or plant the lawn has warmed for a long time (Mossback, Staghorn, Cactuskin,
    Kelpsprite, and the Mower-kin, which grew the Mower's shape).
  - **Dropped:** a Tall One thing that woke (Hoseleech, Clockwork Frog, Rustcrab, Bonsai Titan).
  - **Shaped:** fired clay that was never a gnome: Kiln leavings, glaze scraps, unfinished pieces (Kilnpup, Unfired,
    Frosthound, Kilnwarden).
  - **Gnome:** three pages are gnomes: the Thimblekin (a Feral), the Hedgewright (a logger in hiding) and Gnome
    Jesus, who is in the book because he wrote it.
- **The elements are not new magic.** Rule 7 allows three schools, and Snap keeps to them: **Bloom, Sun and Rain**
  are what the lawn gives. **Clay** and **Iron** are the two *materials* (the code ids stay `clay` and `iron`): Clay
  is earth, burrow-clay and fired clay, the stuff gnomes themselves are made of; Iron is metal that hums, Shard iron or
  Tall One metal that has sat near the hum of the [Blade Shard](../../lore/entries/fescue/blade-shard.md). Neither is
  a school and neither casts anything.
- **Why photographs?** A specimen cannot be kept: you cannot glaze a mole. Gnomes do not know what framing is for, but
  they understand attention. A Gnomokemon that has been seen *properly* settles into its pose. Being seen is the one
  thing a leftover can still be given. The Lab's grading (Size, Framing, Pose, the fifth thing) is a gnome's guess at
  what "properly" means, worked out by trial.

### Loose threads (keep them loose)

- Whether the Gnomokemon are waiting for the Tall Ones, or have forgotten them. Both are in the Gnomokedex.
- What "kemon" means. Gnome Jesus gives three answers and they do not agree: "kin, mostly"; "kept, I think"; "it
  looked good on the sign". (*Proposed lines, not in the game yet.*)
- Whether the Gnomokemon have always been there, or are *more* since the Rumbling. The records say the first; the
  memories say the second. Nobody has counted.

## 2. Where do they come from?

**The short answer:** from the places a Tall One stopped. In most regions, the Gnomokemon are the things that were put
down there: the greenhouse's pots and toys, the shed's tools, the hearth's stones. A region is where the lawn had the
longest time to ask "what are you for?". (The Gutters, the Cistern and the Stopping Road were dug or walked by gnomes;
they have the same question at one remove: *what did we stop doing here?*)

A new region should start from a question: **what was stopped here?** (The Glasshouse: tending. The Dry Yard:
sitting on the porch. The Compost Heap: turning it.)

### Home and also-seen-in

The Gnomokedex lists one *home region* per species. A ride shows some of them somewhere else too. That is not a bug:
a Gnomokemon goes where its wanting goes. The rule the game should follow:

- **Home** is where the Gnomokedex page lives and where it is most common.
- **Also seen in** is anywhere its "wants" line is satisfied. A Kilnpup wants warm hearthstones, so it is also along
  the Stopping Road. A Hoseleech wants pressure and a sunny bank, so it is also in the Deeps and the Cistern. Write
  the reason next to every extra sighting.
- **The Dry Yard is the exception**: it is the Yard's, not Fescue's, so no Fescue-home species is seen there.

The full table is in [regions.md](regions.md#2-home-and-also-seen-in).

**Grubthorn** is a mole king in flight: "leading his clan south, away from the shaking". The ride meets him
wherever and whenever you catch him, never at rest. His Gnomokedex lines are his last words from Gnomomia, so Snap
does not date him.

## 3. Why is Gnome Jesus looking at me?

Gnome Jesus has two jobs in Snap. He **grades** every photo, and he is also **Gnomokedex #036**, a Boss the dex calls a
Mythic, photographable at the end of the Starlight Greenhouse ride. Where the player meets his stare today: the Grading
Office (he sits behind the desk, turned to the player), the grading panel (a centred portrait facing the camera) and the
Lobby. In the Greenhouse he currently *orbits* and does not face the lens; the lore says he stands in one spot every
night and faces it (a follow-up, below).

### What gnomes say

- **The street:** "His eyes follow you round a room." "He knows your score before you have framed the shot." "He says
  he is three thousand years old." "The halo is a Tall-eye."
- **What he says himself:** "I look at everyone. It is a courtesy, and also an audit." (*Proposed line.*)
- **The deed:** the Lab's lot is his, bought outright, in clay ([R-020](../../lore/rulings.md)). The Greenhouse is
  on it. He is standing in his own back garden.
- **Memory:** gnomes at the edge of the lawn remember a bearded gnome in a white robe sketching, the week after the
  First Rumble. He asked every slug what it wanted. He wrote the answers down.
- **His name:** "Gnome Jesus" is the street name, for the robe, the beard and the halo. His given name is **Hyssop**,
  and nobody uses it. He does not preach, takes no tithes and is not a god; lines like "Rejoice!" are vanity, not
  doctrine. (That god is the Gnome Company parody, [R-005](../../lore/rulings.md).)

### The truth

He is looking at you for three reasons, and the third is the true one.

1. **You are holding the Eye-Box**, the Tall One's camera, which he has watched go out and come back with rolls of
   film for thirty years. The camera is the only thing in Fescue that makes a picture of a thing the way a Tall One
   did. To him, looking into it is looking at the Tall Ones' own opinion.
2. **Every page in the Gnomokedex has a photograph, except page thirty-six.** #036 is his. He has graded ten thousand
   photographs and nobody has ever taken an honest one of *him*.
3. **He is posing.** Straight at the lens, the same spot, every night, hoping the next photographer is the one. The
   surprise in his line ("Wait. That is me. That is ME.") is theatre. The *Pose* score rewards a subject facing the
   camera, and he knows it. He is afraid of Bronze.

This is why the grader is also a creature in his own book, and why he waits in his own back garden: the Gnomokedex
is not finished until someone frames him with care and honesty, and he has built a whole Lab so that it can be.

### Design hooks this opens (not built)

- The Greenhouse has an **empty picture frame** propped on the potting bench, facing the door (see
  [regions.md](regions.md#6-starlight-greenhouse-the-glasshouse)): "Page Thirty-Six". The player's best Gnome Jesus
  photo goes in it.
- A Bronze Jesus photo gets a line that is a little too quiet. A Divine one gets him to say nothing at all.
- He never mentions the frame.
- **Code follow-up:** make #036 hold still and face the camera when the camera is steady (today his spawn uses `orbit`,
  which turns him along his circle).

### Loose threads (keep them loose)

- **How old he is.** "Three thousand years of dust" and "even the Mower was jammed for three thousand years" are in
  his lines already. It is either bragging or literal. Say nothing more.
- Who made him.
- Whether the halo is a Tall-eye. Nobody has asked to hold it.

## 4. What does the dog do?

The dog is **Tag**. He carries the camera, the photographer and a carrier gnome on his back along a fixed route
(the ride), and everything about how the ride works is Tag.

### What gnomes say

- **The name:** the dog wears a collar with a tag the size of a cart wheel, scratched with Tall One writing nobody can
  read. The gnomes call him "Tag", for the thing he wears. Nobody has asked what it is short for.
- **The walk:** he walks a round, starting from the Lab's door. The Lab's survey team worked out that if you sit on
  him, you are taken round his route. The ride's rail *is* his walk.
- **The saddle:** an old garden glove (gnomes say "an Empty Hand") stitched to a rope harness by the Lab's weavers.
  The carrier gnome sits in the thumb-pocket and the photographer stands on the carrier's shoulders (R-019).
- **In the Lobby:** he lies on the rug facing the front door, thumps his tail when it opens, then puts his head down
  again. Gnomes say "he is waiting for the walk". Some say he is waiting for someone.

### The truth

Tag is a dog **from the town side**, a Tall One's pet in the Yard, where R-012 already says a dog is a trace of the
Tall Ones and not a sighting of one, and where the Tall Ones are simply never home. He is not Fescue's dog, so Fescue's
Leaving (about 3,000 years ago) is untouched. He is not the Yard's Biscuit and not Gnome Company's Fetch Dog; they only
share a prefab model. What he does in Snap, in order of importance:

1. **He carries the camera.** No gnome can walk the whole route and still frame a shot. The saddle is a camera
   platform, and the ride is a rail because a dog on a habitual route is predictable.
2. **He is the one big thing nobody runs far from.** Most creatures flee gnomes. They have known Tag all their lives:
   to a mole or a moth, Tag is weather. A skittish creature still bolts, but from the *gnomes on his back*. The
   photographer rides in his smell.
3. **His tail sets the beat.** When the Gnome Flute plays, Tag wags, and anything within a few dog-lengths falls into
   step and dances. (The game uses a 12-unit radius from the dog. That is the "wag".) The flute plays *Tag*, and the
   creatures follow him.
4. **He sets the pace.** The Speed control is a pat on the neck. Tag goes as fast as he wants and a pat only
   suggests. Skittish creatures like him slow.
5. **He rests.** Between rides he sleeps on the Lobby rug (R-019). He is never fed on screen. He never speaks. He
   is a very large, patient animal.

### Loose threads (keep them loose)

- Who Tag's person is, and whether they are coming back. He faces the door.
- What the tag says. Gnome Jesus has a theory. He does not share it.
- How his walk gets from the town side into the walled garden. Nobody asks.
- Why his route passes through the Dry Yard: it is on his side of the town.

## 5. The Eye-Box (and the film)

Not asked, but it is the fifth thing in every answer above. The camera is the **Eye-Box**, a Tall One's camera found
in a drawer of the Glasshouse's potting bench, with the film, when the lot came with the glasshouse. It takes a roll of
24 frames per ride. **Nobody in Fescue knows how to make more film.** Jesus says the drawer is deep and he has stopped
counting. He counts at night.

This is the Snap-wide sad note: every ride uses up a roll of something that cannot be made again. The game never
says so. A line or two does.
