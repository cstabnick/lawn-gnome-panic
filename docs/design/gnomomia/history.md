# History of Fescue

Three layers, as [lore-rules.md](lore-rules.md) rule 3 requires: **the Planting** (about 3,000 years ago, myth), **the Charters** (about 300 years ago, history) and **the Rumbling** (about 30 years ago, memory). Each section says what gnomes *believe*, then notes the truth in "Designer's truth" boxes, so writers know what the world is hiding. Players learn the truth only in pieces, and some of it never.

---

## The Planting (about 3,000 years ago): myth

### What the songs say

> *First the Lamp, then the Rain, then the Hand in the soil.*
> *The Hand made the Kiln and the Kiln made the clay*
> *and the clay stood up and was gnome.*
> — opening of "The Long Lawn", sung at every Mossbottom harvest

- **The Tall Ones planted the lawn.** Fescue was bare dirt until the Gardeners seeded it. Every gnome prayer starts with "Tall Ones, who sowed us".
- **The Kiln.** The Gardeners shaped the first gnomes from Rockery clay and fired them in the Kiln, a great hollow in the rocks. Seven gnomes came out first, the **Kiln-born**. The best-known is **Gnorwin Clayfoot**, the first king. (The songs never agree on the other six. Mossbottom's song names a gnome called Moss, and every village claims one.)
- **The Charge.** The Gardeners gave the gnomes one job: *"Keep the lawn."* Gnomes take this to mean tend it, guard it and keep pests from it. Every faction in Fescue disagrees about what "keep" means (see the three ideas in [lore-rules.md](lore-rules.md) rule 10).
- **The Turning Blade.** The Gardeners had a servant beast that lived in the Dark House (the Shed). When the lawn grew too long, they woke it by pulling the **Waking Rope**, and it rolled across Fescue roaring, *drinking Black Dew and breathing stink*, cutting everything to one height. The gnomes hid underground when it came. This was called **a Mowing**.
- **The Leaving.** One day the Gardeners stopped coming. The songs give a dozen reasons: they were angry, they were sad, they went to plant another lawn beyond the Fence, or they are only sleeping. **Nobody knows.**
- **The Last Mowing and the Stopping.** After the Leaving, the Turning Blade woke **with no Hand on the Rope**. It mowed alone for a summer: across the plains (which is why Cloverfield is flat), into the Rockery (where it struck a rock and **broke a piece off its own blade**), and toward the hedges (which were too thick, so the Hedgewood still stands). Gnorwin Clayfoot rode out with a stone carried up from the Kiln, the **Stopping Stone**, and threw it into the Blade's mouth. The Blade jammed and fell silent. The Kiln-born rolled it back into the Shed and barred the door, and Gnorwin raised a Keep on the tallest stone in Fescue, the Birdbath, to watch the Shed forever.

### What the Planting left in the world

| Trace | Where | Used in game |
|---|---|---|
| The Kiln | Rockery | Late quest site; it relights itself in the present (see below) |
| The Blade Shard: the broken piece of the Mower's blade, buried in rock | Rockery, Shard Mine | **All good gnome iron comes from it.** Gnomes mine and smelt a piece of the monster. |
| Cloverfield's flatness | Cloverfield | Farmland, because the Mower flattened it |
| The Hedgewood | north-east | The Mower couldn't cut it. Wildlings call it holy for that reason. |
| Birdbath Keep | centre | Built as a watchtower over the Shed |
| Footprint lakes (Puddlemere, the Basin) | everywhere | "The Tall Ones stepped here" |

> **Designer's truth.** The Stopping Stone did not kill the Mower. It jammed the blade, a rock wedged under a mower deck. Over 3,000 years of the Mower straining in the dark, the Stone has been ground down. Nobody in Fescue knows this. The "monster" is a machine that was left running with a stone in its blade, and it has been chewing that stone slowly ever since.

## The Charters (about 300 years ago): history

Written records start here. The **Royal Archive** in Birdbath Keep holds them, and Archivist Basil Inkcap will tell you three versions of each.

### The Dry Summer (about 310 years ago)

The Spigot (source of the Hose) dropped to a trickle for a whole summer. The lawn browned, crops failed in the plains and moles went deep after the damp. It is the worst famine in written history.

- The Crown founded the **Guild of Pipewrights** to find hidden water. They dug the **Drainpipe Deeps** under Birdbath Keep, a maze of pipes, cisterns and valves that taps the Tall Ones' old buried pipes and can push water anywhere in Fescue. At the bottom is the **Great Valve**.
- The moles lost their damp tunnels to the Pipewrights' digging. **The mole clans' grudge against gnomes dates from this.** (Grubthorn's great-grandmother, Dowager Mudtooth, still remembers it. Moles live long too.)

### The Dandelion Schism (about 305 years ago)

During the Dry Summer, some gnomes said the drought was the lawn punishing gnomes for cutting it. They let their plots go to dandelion and clover and refused the Crown's tending laws. King Bartholomew Clayfoot III exiled them.

- They went to the Hedgewood and became the **Wildlings** ("let it grow").
- A splinter of the Crown's own Groundskeepers went the other way: they decided the Dry Summer happened because the lawn had grown *uneven*, and that the Mower had been holy, a cleanser. They became the **Order of the Even Cut** ("cut it all"). The Crown tolerates them as cranks. They keep a chapel in Thatchford and a fort on the Shorn, the strip of stubble by the Verge.
- **This is where the three ideas come from.** The Crown, the Wildlings and the Even Cut all date their founding to the same terrible summer.

### The Three Charters (about 300 years ago)

To feed Fescue after the drought, Bartholomew III signed three charters, each giving a guild a region to work forever:

| Charter | Guild | Region | Duty to the Crown |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Charter of Root and Seed | Fieldwardens | **Cloverfield** | Grain, honeydew, snail-carts |
| The Charter of Bough | Hedgewrights | **Hedgewood** | Timber, game, bowstring |
| The Charter of Stone | Delvers | **The Rockery** | Iron, slate, Tall-eyes |

This is why each industry region exists and why each one has a charter-hall, a guild boss and a tithe to the Keep. The Hedgewood charter is the sore one. It gave the Hedgewrights the right to log the forest **where the Wildlings had just settled**, and that fight has gone on for 300 years.

### Thatchford is founded (about 290 years ago)

A market grew where the Hose leaks across the road, the only ford between the south villages and the Keep. Grain from Cloverfield, timber from the Hedgewood and iron from the Rockery all crossed it. **Thatchford is a crossroads town and lives by trade.**

> **Designer's truth.** The Dry Summer wasn't the lawn's anger. A Tall One tap was turned almost off. Who turned it is a loose thread. The Pipewrights' Great Valve can, in fact, open that old buried pipe wide. That is what makes the Valve matter in the final battle.

## The Rumbling (about 30 years ago): memory

Living gnomes remember this, and none of them remember it the same way.

### The First Rumble (32 years ago)

On a still night, all of Fescue heard a sound like a giant clearing its throat, once, from the north. Cups fell off shelves in Thatchford. Miners in the Shard Mine said the iron *hummed*. The next morning there was a new crack in the Shed's door.

- The Even Cut called it **the Saint Stirring** and doubled their numbers in a year.
- The Crown sent **King Gnorbert Clayfoot** to the Verge to inspect the Shed. He found the door cracked but holding, and he ordered it re-barred with Rockery iron.

### The Backflow (31 years ago)

To keep the Mower's damp at bay (the Archive says rust "tames" it), the Crown ordered the Pipewrights to flood the Shorn through the Great Valve. Lord Chancellor **Wendel Potsworth**, then a junior treasurer, cut the cost of the job by having the valve seals made of clay instead of Shard iron. The seals failed. The Deeps **flooded backward**. Forty Pipewrights were trapped below. Master Pipewright **Gromwell** stayed at the Great Valve to close it by hand so the water wouldn't reach the Keep. He was never seen again. The lower Deeps were sealed and have been full of pests ever since.

- **Ida Gromwell**, his daughter, is now Master Pipewright. She has never forgiven the Crown, and she doesn't know about the cheap seals.
- **Potsworth** knows exactly what he did. That guilt is why he insists the Mower is a fairy tale: if the Mower is real, the Backflow was a real failure of a real duty, and it was his.

### The King's Fall (30 years ago)

King Gnorbert went back to the Verge to check his iron bars. On the way home his escort was ambushed on the Shorn. The Crown says Wildlings did it. The Wildlings say the Even Cut did it. The one survivor, **Knight-Commander Rowan Ironsod** (then a squire), says the attackers wore *no hats*, and he won't say more. The King was shattered past mending.

- His daughter **Marigold** was crowned at 8 years old. Chancellor Potsworth ruled as regent for 20 years and still runs most of the realm.
- The Crown/Wildling feud became open hatred. Hedgewrights log harder, and Wildlings raid lumber camps.

> **Designer's truth.** The ambush was the Even Cut, led by a young Edger named **Sedgewick**, who wanted the Crown's iron off the Shed door. They went bareheaded so the attack would be blamed on Wildlings, who often go bareheaded. Sedgewick is now **High Edger** and an "adviser" at court. Rowan suspects, but he has no proof. The player can earn the proof in the Verge chapter (quest "No Hats", see [quests.md](quests.md)).

### The Quiet Years (30 years ago to now)

Nothing happened, and that was the problem. The Rumble didn't come back, so most gnomes stopped believing in it. The young went to the Keep for work. Sodbury's farms emptied. The Shed's iron bars rusted. The Even Cut quietly took the Shorn.

## Now: why the present looks the way it does

| What the player sees | Why (from the layers above) |
|---|---|
| **Grubthorn the Mole King tunnels under Mossbottom** | Moles feel the ground before gnomes do. The mole clans are fleeing south away from the Mower's growing shudder, and Mossbottom is in the way. Grubthorn's dying line: *"We weren't attacking. We were running."* |
| **Feral gnomes camp outside Mossbottom** | Wildlings so bitter after the King's Fall feud that even Mother Burdock threw them out. They took "let it grow" to "take everything". |
| **Beetle Barrows are crawling** | Stag beetles bury their young in rotting wood, and the Hedgewrights' log-dumping 30 years ago filled the barrows with it. |
| **Thatchford's Spinning Fountains spray at random** | The Deeps' pipes have been leaking pressure since the Backflow. |
| **Cloverfield is half-empty** | The young left during the Quiet Years. Red ants moved onto abandoned ranches and rustle aphids. |
| **Wildlings and Hedgewrights are at war** | The Charter of Bough (300 years ago) plus the King's Fall (30 years ago). |
| **The Rockery's iron hums** | It is a piece of the Mower's blade. It hums when the Mower moves. |
| **The Kiln lit itself four nights ago** | Nobody lit it. Kilnkeeper Sedum is terrified. It went out again the next morning. |
| **You washed up in Mossbottom's brook three days ago, clutching an old trowel** | The brook is fed by the Hose, which comes down from the Spigot in the Rockery, right beside the Kiln. **Loose thread.** The trowel has the Clayfoot mark (a footprint in clay) under the rust. |

### What is happening now

The Stopping Stone is nearly ground to dust. The Mower is starting to turn again. Thirty years ago it slipped once (the First Rumble). This year it slips every few nights, and the Shed door is splitting. When the Stone breaks, the Turning Blade will roll out across the Verge and **mow Fescue down to the dirt**, beginning with the Even Cut fort that welcomes it.

The player's journey is to rebuild what Gnorwin did with what the Charters built: water from the Pipewrights, timber from the Hedgewrights, stone from the Delvers and carts from the Fieldwardens. It ends with the **Battle of the Verge**.

### Loose threads (never fully answered in Fescue)

- Who is the player? A Kiln-born gnome fired when the Mower stirred? A lost Clayfoot? Only hints.
- Who turned the Spigot down during the Dry Summer?
- Why did the Tall Ones leave, and are they coming back?
- What is beyond the Fence? (Other lawns. The next world is a set-up, not an answer.)
- After the Battle of the Verge the Mower is stopped, not destroyed. Its Belly is torn open, and something that isn't grass is inside. (Sequel hook.)
