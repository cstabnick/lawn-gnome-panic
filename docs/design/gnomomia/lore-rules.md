# Lore rules

The short rulebook for anything written about Gnomomia: places, people, items, quests, barks. When a new piece of lore breaks one of these rules, change the piece, not the rule (or argue for the rule change in this file first).

## 1. Everything is garden-sized

The world is a back garden seen from a gnome's height. Every big landmark is an ordinary yard object.

| What it really is | What gnomes call it |
|---|---|
| A stone birdbath | Birdbath Keep, the royal castle (its bowl is "the Basin") |
| A rock garden | The Rockery, the mountains |
| A garden hose | The Hose, the great green river |
| The outdoor tap | The Spigot, where all water comes from |
| Sprinkler heads | The Spinning Fountains |
| A compost heap | The Compost Heap (gnomes got this one right) |
| The picket fence | The Fence, the edge of the world |
| The garden shed | The Shed, the Dark House |
| The lawnmower | The Mower, the Turning Blade, Old Rumble |
| Petrol | Black Dew |
| The spark plug | The Heartspark |
| The pull cord | The Waking Rope |
| The grass bag | The Belly |
| Glass marbles | Tall-eyes (the most precious gems) |
| A thimble, a pot lid, a button | Armor, shields and coin |

**Rule:** the player should recognise the object, but the gnomes never do. No gnome ever says "hose", "mower" or "birdbath" knowing what it is for. ("Birdbath" is just an old name nobody can explain.)

## 2. Nobody ever sees the Tall Ones

The gardeners who made the gnomes are called **the Tall Ones** or **the Gardeners**. They are never shown, never heard and never explained. Their traces are huge (footprints that became lakes, a dropped trowel the size of a bridge), but no trace ever settles who they were or whether they will come back. No humans appear on screen, ever.

## 3. History comes in threes

Every place, faction and grudge sits on three layers of time. Each layer is a different *kind* of truth:

| Layer | When | What kind of truth | How the player hears it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Planting** | about 3,000 years ago | Myth. Told in songs and sayings that contradict each other. Probably half wrong. | Songs, carvings, prayers, children's rhymes, item flavour text |
| **The Charters** | about 300 years ago | History. Written records exist, but the factions disagree on *why* things happened. | Books, the Royal Archive, statues, place names, old laws |
| **The Rumbling** | about 30 years ago | Memory. Living gnomes were there, and they have personal grudges and guilt. | NPC dialogue, first-hand stories, things people won't talk about |

**Rule:** a major place needs a reason from at least two layers. A town is never "just there". Something in the past put it there, and something in living memory changed it.

**Rule:** the layers never fully agree. Myth says one thing, the records say something close to it, and a living gnome remembers it differently. Leave the gap. The player gets to work out what really happened, and sometimes nobody knows.

## 4. Gnomes are fired clay

- Gnomes were shaped and fired in **the Kiln** in the Rockery. They are clay with a glaze, and they are proud of a good glaze.
- Gnomes do not die easily. They **crack**. A badly cracked gnome is "shattered", which is death. Small cracks are glued and re-glazed. That is what the Bloom school of healing is (Mend = glazing a crack shut).
- **Respawning has a reason in the lore.** When the player falls, they are carried to the nearest **Glaze Shrine** (in Mossbottom, the well) and glued back together. NPCs can comment on it ("Cracked again? You're more glue than gnome.").
- Gnomes live about 150 to 200 years. Elder Fernwick (212) is the oldest gnome alive. Nobody alive saw the Charters era. Plenty of gnomes saw the Rumbling.
- New gnomes are not born. They are *made*: today by kilnwrights in small town kilns, never as well as the old Kiln made them. Gnomes from the old Kiln (the "Kiln-born") were said to be stronger. None are known to be alive.

## 5. Names: plain, garden, at most one pun

- **Worlds** are named after grass species: **Fescue** (this one), and later ones beyond the Fence such as Bluegrass, Ryegrass, Zoysia and Bermuda. This is the only rule that uses botany jargon.
- **Places** join two plain garden words: Mossbottom, Thatchford, Sodbury, Flintdelve, Barkhollow, Clippings, Last Tuft. Real English place endings (-ford, -bury, -ton, -wick, -delve, -hollow) are good. **One pun per name at most, and zero is fine.** "Thatchford" is right; "Mowtown" is too much.
- **Gnome first names** are plants: Fernwick, Tansy, Bramble, Thistle, Marigold, Sorrel, Yarrow, Sedum, Burdock. Plants from the gnome's region suit best (rock-garden plants in the Rockery, grasses and clovers in Cloverfield).
- **Gnome surnames** are soil, tools and household things: Clayfoot, Potsworth, Burlap, Stoutpot, Hammerclod, Splitwell, Haybale.
- **Pests and beasts** get grubby, simple names: Grubthorn, Mudtooth, Old Gleaner, Clogmaw.
- **Titles** are garden jobs: Reeve, Edger, Kilnkeeper, Pipewright, Weeder, Groundskeeper.

## 6. Every pest wants something

Monsters are real garden creatures: slugs, moles, beetles, ants, crows, wasps, spiders, earwigs, rats. **None of them are evil for no reason.** Moles are fleeing something. Ants are ranchers like the gnomes. The crow is hungry. The wasps are defending their nest. The player still fights them, but the quest text says what they want. The only thing in Fescue with no want anyone can name is the Mower.

## 7. Magic is what a lawn needs

There are three schools, and each is something a garden needs to grow:

- **Sun**: heat and light (fire, burning, bursts). The old "Destruction" school.
- **Rain**: water and cold (frost, slows, storms, control).
- **Bloom**: soil and growth (healing, glazing, roots, shields). The old "Restoration" school.

There is no fourth school. Necromancy, demons, arcane runes and lightning-from-nowhere don't exist. A storm is Rain, and lightning is a Rain trick at most.

## 8. The only engine in the world is the Mower

Gnome technology is clay, wood, stone, string, scavenged Tall One junk and water pressure (the Pipewrights). There is no gunpowder, no steam engine and nothing that roars. **The Mower is the only machine that runs by itself**, and that is why it terrifies everyone. Nobody in Fescue knows what an engine is. Gnome descriptions of the Mower are animal and holy: it *drinks*, it *breathes stink*, it *wakes when the Rope is pulled*.

## 9. Tone: cozy on top, one honest sad note per region

- The surface is warm, silly and dry. Jokes are understated: Fernwick's beard "has its own postcode". There are no pop-culture references and no fourth-wall jokes.
- **Every region carries one real sad thing**, played straight: a gnome who lost someone to the Backflow, a village emptied by the young leaving, a clay gnome that never finished firing. Don't milk it, and don't joke over it.
- Danger is real but never gory. Things crack, scatter, flee and get mowed. Nothing bleeds red. A "bleed" effect is sap or clay dust.

## 10. Three ideas in tension

Every faction in Fescue answers one question: **how should the lawn grow?**

| Idea | Who | They believe |
|---|---|---|
| **Tend it** | The Crown, most towns | Cut a little, water a little, keep the balance. The Gardeners' old way. |
| **Let it grow** | The Wildlings (and the Ferals, who took it too far) | Cutting is cruelty. The wild should take the lawn back. |
| **Cut it all** | The Order of the Even Cut | Only a perfectly level lawn is holy. The Mower is a saint, not a monster. |

A new faction, NPC or quest should lean toward one of these three. That gives every conflict in Fescue the same backbone, so the final battle feels like the answer to a question the world has been asking all along.

## 11. Leave threads loose

Some things stay unexplained. Where did the player come from? Who lit the Kiln? Are the Tall Ones coming back? What is beyond the Fence? Write hints, never answers. A quest may end with "nobody knows", and that is a fine ending.

## Checklist for a new piece of lore

1. Is it a garden object at gnome scale, and does the gnome name sound like a gnome named it? (Rules 1, 8)
2. Does it have roots in at least two of the three time layers? (Rule 3)
3. Does the name follow the naming rule, with one pun at most? (Rule 5)
4. If it is a creature, what does it want? (Rule 6)
5. Which of the three ideas does it lean toward? (Rule 10)
6. Did it give away a loose thread? If so, take that out. (Rule 11)
