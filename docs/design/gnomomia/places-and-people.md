# Places and people

One sheet per zone. Each sheet has the zone's reason for existing (from the [history](history.md) layers), its sub-areas, its town spec (services, layout) and its people. Each person has a **role**, a **want**, a **secret or wound**, and a sample line. Quest ids point to [quests.md](quests.md). Enemies are in [bestiary.md](bestiary.md).

Town service legend: **Shrine** (respawn), **Smith**, **Herbalist**, **General** (food, bags), **Trainer** (class skills, respec), **Bank**, **Inn** (rest XP), **Cart** (snail-cart fast travel), **Board** (bounty board with repeatable kill quests).

## 1. Mossbottom Vale (levels 1-6), the start

**Why it's here.** *Planting:* the vale is a footprint hollow. The songs say a Tall One knelt here to plant the first mushrooms, which is why Mossbottom's houses are mushrooms. *Charters:* never chartered and never taxed much, so it's a backwater that grows its own food. *Rumbling:* barely touched by it, until this month.

**Sad note.** Old Thistle started sweeping the path forty years ago, when his wife Primrose took work in the Keep's waterworks: it was the path she walked home on, every season. She was lost in the Backflow nine years later. He never stopped sweeping. He never says why. Tansy will tell you if you ask her about him.

### Mossbottom (town)
Five mushroom houses around a well. Services: **Shrine** (the well), **Smith** (Bramble), **Herbalist** (Tansy), Board (added after the Mole King). No trainer; the player is classless until Thatchford.

| Person | Role | Want | Secret / wound | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Elder Fernwick** (212) | Village elder, quest hub | For Mossbottom to stay small and quiet | He is the last gnome alive who met Bartholomew III's grandson, and he has always believed the Mower is real. He sends the player north because he knows what Grubthorn's flight means. | "My knees hurt when the ground shakes. My knees have been hurting a great deal." |
| **Bramble the Smith** | Smith | Real Shard iron; he only gets scraps | He did his apprenticeship in Flintdelve and was thrown out for drinking. He'd love to go back. | "Bronze? Bronze is for buttons. Bring me Rockery iron and I'll show you a blade." |
| **Tansy the Herbalist** | Potions, healing | To study in the Royal Archive | She writes letters to the Archivist every week, and nobody writes back. | "If it's green, I can boil it. If it's purple, I won't." |
| **Captain Pip** | Mossbottom guard (he *is* the guard) | To be taken seriously by the Keep | He was a Keep recruit who washed out. He keeps the rejection letter. | "Halt! ...Oh. Carry on. Halt *politely*, I meant." |
| **Old Thistle** | Sweeper, tutorial voice | To keep the path clean | Primrose; see the sad note. | "In my day we fought slugs bare-handed. Uphill." |
| **Moss Kiln-Daughter** (statue) | The well's carved figure | – | Mossbottom's claim to one of the seven Kiln-born. The player may *Examine* it: "Her face is worn smooth. She is holding a trowel just like yours." | – |

### Sub-areas
- **Dewdrop Meadow** (1-2): slugs, moles. Morning mist.
- **Puddlemere** (2): footprint lake. Frogs on the shore (passive, 2). A drowned old cart holds a hidden chest.
- **Feral Camp** (3-4): feral gnomes, stolen garden furniture, a crude idol of a dandelion.
- **Beetle Barrows** (4-5): stag beetle mounds full of rotten logs. A Hedgewright stamp is still visible on the logs, a clue for later.
- **Grubthorn's Burrow** (6 elite): the Mole King. Collapses after the fight and opens the north road.

## 2. Thatchford Downs (levels 6-11), the market and your Calling

**Why it's here.** *Planting:* the Hose runs here, and where it leaks across the low ground it makes the only ford for miles. *Charters:* founded about 290 years ago as the crossroads for the three charters' goods. *Rumbling:* the First Rumble cracked the old mill wheel, and the Spinning Fountains started firing at random after the Backflow, which keeps half the downs soggy.

**Sad note.** The Puddleby inn has a table nobody sits at. It is laid for the forty Pipewrights lost in the Backflow, many of them Thatchford gnomes. A toast is made to it every night. If the player sits there, the room goes quiet.

### Thatchford (town)
A real town of about forty buildings on both banks, joined by a stone ford. It has thatched toadstool roofs, a market square, a mill and a guildhall.

Services: **Shrine** (fountain in the square), **Smith**, **Herbalist**, **General**, **Trainers ×3** (in the guildhall), **Bank**, **Inn** (*The Tipsy Toadstool*), **Board**, **Cart** (after Cloverfield).

| Person | Role | Want | Secret / wound | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Reeve Yarrow Burlap** | Town leader | Trade to keep flowing; no panic | Owes the Chancellor money and quietly does as Potsworth says. Will try to bury the player's Mower warning. | "A rumble? Carts. It's always carts." |
| **Sergeant Briar Stoutpot** | **Warden trainer** | Recruits who don't cry | Served with Knight-Commander Ironsod; was on the King's escort but sent home sick the day before. Guilty that she lived. | "Plant your feet. A gnome who's planted can't be moved." |
| **Hazel Twitchett** | **Slinger trainer** | Bigger, faster acorns | A Barkhollow hunter who ran off with a Wildling and came back alone. | "Don't aim at the beetle. Aim at where the beetle's going to be." |
| **Magister Sorrel Dimm** | **Hedge-Mage trainer** | Proof that the Kiln is magic | Has a sliver of Blade Shard he stole from the Archive and listens to it hum at night. | "Sun, Rain, Bloom. Everything else is just weather." |
| **Dill Puddleby** | Innkeeper | Gossip | Knows which merchants are Even Cut. Will sell that for a favour. | "Rest XP's on the house. Ale isn't." |
| **Rye Grindwell** | Miller | His wheel fixed | The wheel cracked on the night of the First Rumble. He has kept a tally of every tremor since, and it is speeding up. **(A key clue for the Reeve quest.)** | "Thirty-one years of tallies. Count them. Then tell me it's carts." |
| **Nettle** | Pipewright apprentice | To fix the Spinning Fountains | She is Ida Gromwell's apprentice and will be your link to the Deeps. | "Pressure's wrong. Pressure's been wrong since before I was glazed." |

### Sub-areas
- **The Hose banks** (6-7): water beetles, frogs, leeches.
- **The Spinning Fountains** (7-8): a field of sprinkler heads. Environmental hazard: any one may burst and knock you back (a telegraphed ring). Muck sprites (sentient puddles). Rats.
- **Crowfoot Hill** (8-9): a crow's old roost, now taken over by magpie thieves (shiny-loot stealers).
- **The Compost Heap** (9-11, **mini-dungeon**): rats, worms, grubs, and the boss **Rotgut, Rat of the Heap**. Warm, steaming, disgusting. The rats are fat and organised: a rat kingdom built on gnome waste.
- **The Neat Chapel** (neutral): an Even Cut chapel with a perfectly trimmed lawn. Cutting a single blade of their grass turns them hostile in this area only (a joke, and a warning).

## 3. Birdbath Keep (hub), the capital

**Why it's here.** *Planting:* the Birdbath is the tallest stone in Fescue. Gnorwin built his watchtower on it to see the Shed. *Charters:* Bartholomew III expanded it into a palace and dug the Drainpipe Deeps beneath it. *Rumbling:* the King died, and the Chancellor has ruled through a child-queen for 30 years.

**Sad note.** Queen Marigold has never been allowed to leave the Keep since her father's death. She has never seen the Verge, the Hedgewood or a real farm. She watches the robins land in the Basin and asks visitors what outside smells like.

### Layout
- **The Pedestal**: the castle itself, carved into the birdbath's stone column. Throne hall, Archive, barracks, the Chancellor's counting room.
- **The Basin**: the bowl on top, a royal lake. Robins land here. The **Robin Roost** is on its rim.
- **Pedestal Ward**: the town around the base. Guild halls of all three charters, the bank, and the stairs to the **Drainpipe Deeps**.

Services: **Shrine**, **Smith** (royal armoury, best gear in the mid-game), **Herbalist**, **General**, **Trainers ×3** (advanced ranks), **Bank**, **Inn** (*The Basin Rim*), **Board**, **Cart**, **Robin flight** (to Last Tuft, late game).

| Person | Role | Want | Secret / wound | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Queen Marigold Clayfoot** (38) | Monarch | To be a real queen; to see her kingdom | She has secretly been reading Gnorwin's legends and believes every word. She is the player's only ally at court at first. | "Tell me: does Cloverfield really smell of honey?" |
| **Lord Chancellor Wendel Potsworth** | Regent in all but name | The Mower to stay a myth | **He cut the costs that caused the Backflow.** See history. Not evil: a coward who has spent 30 years hiding one mistake. Can be redeemed at the end. | "Myths are expensive. The treasury isn't." |
| **Archivist Basil Inkcap** | Keeper of records | Someone to *read* the records | Owes Tansy fifty-two replies he never got (because the post office held her letters; see Clementine). Delighted when you fix it. | "There are three accounts of that. All wrong in interesting ways." |
| **Knight-Commander Rowan Ironsod** | Head of the army | Justice for the King | The lone survivor of the King's Fall. Knows the ambushers were bareheaded. Suspects Sedgewick. | "I was a squire. I hid under the King's cart. I have been hiding ever since." |
| **Master Pipewright Ida Gromwell** | Head of the Pipewrights; keeper of the Deeps | Her father found | Doesn't know about the cheap seals. When she learns, she wants Potsworth shattered. The player's choice can calm her (or not). | "Water goes where you tell it. Gnomes don't." |
| **High Edger Sedgewick** | Even Cut leader, "adviser" at court | The Mower freed | Led the King's Fall ambush. Calm, polite and patient, he believes the Mower is a saint that will make Fescue perfect. **Main villain of the gnome side.** | "When it comes, it will not hate you. It will simply make you even." |
| **Robin-Master Wren Tumble** | Keeper of the Royal Robins | Her birds not used for war | Will fly the player to the Verge only once the Queen orders it. | "A robin is not a horse. A robin is a robin." |

## 3a. The Drainpipe Deeps (dungeon under the Keep)

**Why it's here.** Dug in the Dry Summer, broken by the Backflow, sealed for 30 years. Each wing opens with a different main-story chapter, so the player comes back three times and goes deeper each time.

| Wing | Levels | Opened by | Theme | Boss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Gutters** | 10-12 | Chapter 3 (find Gnorwin's Ledger) | Upper pipes, ankle-deep water, rats, leeches | **Clogmaw**, a living blockage of hair, roots and slime that sucks you in |
| **The Cistern** | 18-20 | Chapter 5 (find the old Valve maps) | Huge drowned hall, pillars, a sunken Pipewright barracks | **Mother Vesh**, a giant leech queen, and her brood |
| **The Backflow** | 24-25 | Chapter 6 (open the Great Valve) | The flooded bottom, rust everywhere, the forty lost Pipewrights' tools | **Gromwell, Who Holds the Valve**: the cracked, rust-bound Pipewright still at his post after 30 years. He doesn't attack until you touch the Valve. **The emotional climax of the Keep storyline.** |

The Gromwell fight: he isn't evil. He has held the Valve shut for 30 years and thinks you are the flood. Defeat him, or (via a hidden talk option, if Ida came with you) let her speak to him. Either way he finally lets go, and he says one last line: *"Is it dry up there? Good. Good."*

## 4. Cloverfield (levels 11-16), farming and ranching

**Why it's here.** *Planting:* the Last Mowing flattened these plains, which is why it is the only flat land in Fescue. *Charters:* the Charter of Root and Seed made it Fescue's breadbasket under the **Fieldwardens**. *Rumbling:* during the Quiet Years the young left for the Keep. Half the ranches are empty, and the Red Ants moved into them.

**Theme.** A gnome Western. Ranchers herd **aphids** (milked for honeydew, the gnome staple) and **snails** (draft animals for the snail-carts). Red ants are rival ranchers, rustlers and squatters. A great crow hunts from above.

**Sad note.** Sodbury's schoolhouse has one pupil left. The teacher still rings the bell every morning.

### Sodbury (town)
A farm town with a granary tower (a stacked flowerpot), a snail-pen corral and wide streets. Services: **Shrine**, **Smith**, **General**, **Inn** (*The Full Trough*), **Board**, **Cart** (*the main cart depot; unlocks fast travel*), **Stables** (riding snail mount: +30% walk speed, unlocked by quest).

| Person | Role | Want | Secret / wound | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ma Honeydew Haybale** | Head of the biggest aphid ranch; Fieldwarden elder | Her herds back from the ants | Her three children all left for the Keep and none write. | "An aphid ain't smart, but it's loyal. More than I can say for some." |
| **Sheriff Timothy Fallow** | Law in Cloverfield | The Red Ants run off the old Pettigrew ranch | His deputy was taken by the crow last spring. He won't say her name. | "Ants don't sleep. So I don't either." |
| **Fieldwarden Clover Muckle** | Charter-holder, runs the granary | The Keep's tithe lowered | Has been skimming grain to feed the empty farms' old folk. Technically a crime. | "The Crown wants a tenth. The Crown doesn't plough." |
| **Mabel Snailwright** | Snail-cart builder | The Great Haul (see quests) | Built the cart King Gnorbert rode to the Verge. It came back empty. | "Slow is safe. Fast is how you end up on the Shorn." |
| **The Thousand** (red ant colony) | Rival ranchers | Pasture, aphids | They aren't villains: they need pasture too. A late side quest makes peace possible (shared grazing), which gives an ant contingent in the final battle. | (click-trill noises; a translator NPC, *Antenna-Sister Kree*, speaks for them) |

### Sub-areas
- **Honeydew Ranch** (11-12): aphids (passive), ladybirds (aphid hunters, hostile), fence repair.
- **Snail Pens** (12): runaway snails to lasso. Comic.
- **The Red Anthills** (13-15): soldier ants, worker ants, the **Ant Queen's** mound (elite, optional; peace or war).
- **Scarecrow Rise** (14-16): a Tall One relic, the **Straw King**, a scarecrow. Crows avoid it, so gnomes revere it. Straw-wights (animated straw) haunt it. The Archive says it was "a Gardener who forgot to leave".
- **Crow's Perch** (15-16, **world boss**): a fallen fence post where **Old Gleaner**, the great crow, roosts. The Gleaner is hungry, not evil. Defeat it, or feed it from Fieldwarden Muckle's hidden grain store (the peaceful option) and it leaves, and the Sheriff finds his deputy's hat in the nest.

## 5. Hedgewood (levels 15-20), lumber and hunting

**Why it's here.** *Planting:* the Mower couldn't cut it. The hedges are the only "uncut" land in Fescue, and Wildlings call them holy for it. *Charters:* the Charter of Bough gave the Hedgewrights the right to log here, **on top of the Wildlings' land**. *Rumbling:* after the King's Fall was blamed on Wildlings, the Crown let the Hedgewrights log twice as hard. It is an open war now.

**Theme.** Gnome lumberjacks versus forest folk, with hunters caught in between. Dark hedge tunnels, spiders, a wasp tower. The zone's question is whether the player cuts the Heartwood or is given it.

**Sad note.** Wildling children carve their names into the Elder Privet's bark. The oldest names are 300 years old. The newest names belong to the children the Hedgewrights' falling trees killed.

### Clippings (lumber camp, Crown side)
Log cabins, a sawpit, piles of clippings (the camp is named for the huge heaps of cut branches). Services: **Shrine**, **Smith**, **General**, **Board**, **Cart**.

### Barkhollow (hunters' lodge, neutral)
A lodge in a hollow root. Hunters deal with both sides. Services: **Shrine**, **Trainer** (Slinger advanced; beast taming), **Inn** (*The Antler & Acorn*), **Tanner**.

| Person | Role | Want | Secret / wound | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Boss Alder Splitwell** | Hedgewright foreman | The Heartwood felled; Wildlings gone | His son ran off to join the Wildlings. | "Trees grow back. Gnomes don't." |
| **Juniper Quickstring** | Huntmaster of Barkhollow | Hunters left alone by both sides | She is Hazel Twitchett's lost love, the one who stayed. | "I hunt what's hungry before it hunts what's mine." |
| **Mother Burdock** | Wildling chieftain | The logging stopped forever | She exiled the Ferals herself. She knows the King's Fall wasn't Wildlings, and she is bitter that nobody asked. | "You cut, you call it tending. We grow, you call it wild." |
| **Fen Splitwell** | Burdock's adopted son, Alder's real son | His father and mother-in-the-wood to stop fighting | Will be the go-between in the peace route. | "Da thinks I was stolen. I walked." |
| **Old Lichen** | Wildling elder, half-moss | – | Remembers one of the forty Pipewrights who fled here after the Backflow, and hid him. (He lived; a side quest finds his grave.) | "Slow down, sapling. The hedge has been here longer than your worry." |

### Sub-areas
- **Sawdust Mill** (15-16): Crown mill under Wildling raids. Defend it, or don't.
- **Webhollow** (16-18): spider tunnels. **The Weaver Below** (elite).
- **Burdock's Tangle** (Wildling home; neutral, or hostile if you sided with the Hedgewrights).
- **The Paper Spire** (18-20, **mini-dungeon**): a wasp nest tower. Wasps, drones, and **Queen Vespa**. Both Wildlings and Hedgewrights want it gone, so it's the quest that brings them to the same table.
- **The Elder Privet**: the oldest hedge. The **Heartwood** is in its core. You need one branch of it for the great stake that breaks the Mower's Heartspark.

**The choice.** Chapter 5 offers two ways to get the Heartwood: help Splitwell fell it (fast, angers the Wildlings, and there are no Wildlings in the final battle), or kill Queen Vespa for Burdock, broker the peace, and be *given* a branch (slower, better rewards, and Wildling archers join the final battle). The main story suggests peace but doesn't force it.

## 6. The Rockery (levels 19-24), mining and the Kiln

**Why it's here.** *Planting:* the Kiln is here, where gnomes were made. The Mower broke its blade on these rocks, and the **Blade Shard** is buried in them. The Spigot is here too. *Charters:* the Charter of Stone gave the **Delvers** the mines. They found the Shard and have smelted it into gnome iron for 300 years. *Rumbling:* the Shard **hummed** at the First Rumble. Lately it hums every few nights. Four nights before the player arrived in Mossbottom, **the Kiln lit itself**.

**Theme.** Deep, old, holy and a bit frightening. Miners who know they are mining a monster's tooth. The game's quiet mystery (where the player came from) lives here.

**Sad note.** **The Unfired.** In the Kiln's back chambers stand rows of clay gnomes that were shaped but never fired: soft, half-finished, eyes painted on. Some move. They aren't evil. They want to be finished. When you defeat one, it slumps back into clay with a sigh. The Kilnkeeper visits them every evening.

### Flintdelve (town)
A mining town cut into the rock face, with lift-baskets on pulleys, slate roofs and the Delvers' charter-hall. Services: **Shrine**, **Smith** (*the best smiths in Fescue; Shard-iron gear*), **General**, **Trainer** (Warden advanced), **Bank**, **Inn** (*The Hot Seam*), **Board**, **Cart**, **Leaf-raft dock** (one way down the Hose to Thatchford).

| Person | Role | Want | Secret / wound | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Forewoman Saxifraga "Sax" Hammerclod** | Head of the Delvers | Keep the mine open, whatever it hums | She stopped the mine three times this year and hid it from the Keep, which would have had to stop the iron tithe. | "Iron's iron. If it sings while I dig, I dig louder." |
| **Kilnkeeper Sedum** | Keeper of the Kiln (a hereditary, mostly ceremonial job) | To know who lit the Kiln | She saw it burning and saw a shape walk out of it toward the Spigot. She has told nobody, and she tells the player only near the end. | "It's never been lit in my lifetime. Or my mother's. Or hers." |
| **Grout** | A blind old miner | – | Can hear the Mower from the deep seam. Describes it like a heartbeat. (A pure lore NPC.) | "Thump. Turn. Thump. It's further along than last month." |
| **Bramble's old master, Smith Cobalt Anvilroot** | Master smith | A smith worthy of Shard iron | Kicked Bramble out, regrets it. (Side quest reunites them, and Bramble's shop in Mossbottom upgrades.) | "He had good hands and a bad thirst." |
| **Dowager Mudtooth** | Ancient mole matriarch, Grubthorn's great-grandmother | Her clan safe from the shaking | She remembers the Pipewrights stealing the moles' damp 300 years ago. She will help if you bring news of Grubthorn honestly. | "My great-grandson ran south. You killed him. Tell me true and I'll show you the deep way." |

### Sub-areas
- **The Spigot** (19-20): the source of the Hose, a huge metal tap rising from the rocks. Earwigs. Lore: *the water comes from somewhere else entirely*.
- **Marble Pits** (20-21): Tall-eyes (glass marbles) are dug here. Gem beetles, magpies.
- **Shard Mine** (21-22): iron humming in the walls, centipedes, rock-lice. The deep seam where the Shard's edge is exposed is a **mirror-bright surface the size of a hall**.
- **Mudtooth Deeps** (22-23): mole clan tunnels. Fight or talk.
- **The Kiln** (23-24, **dungeon**): the Unfired, Kiln guardians (fired-clay golems left by the Kiln-born), and the boss **The Kiln Warden**, a giant fired-clay gnome statue that tests anyone who wants to use the Kiln. Its lines suggest it recognises the player's trowel. Beating it lets Sedum fire the **new Stopping Stone**.

## 7. The Verge (levels 25-30), the war front

**Why it's here.** *Planting:* the strip between the lawn and the Fence, where the Mower comes from. *Charters:* the Crown kept a watch-line here. It was abandoned in the Quiet Years, and the Even Cut moved in and mowed their **Shorn**, a stubble desert they keep cut by hand as worship. *Rumbling:* King Gnorbert fell here.

**Theme.** Open, bleak and windy. Everything is cut short, so there's nowhere to hide. Even Cut zealots, things that fled the Shed, and the Mower's shudder getting louder every day. This is where every thread meets.

**Sad note.** The King's cart is still on the Shorn where he fell. The Even Cut keep the grass around it mowed perfectly, as a trophy.

### Last Tuft (forward camp)
The last tuft of uncut grass before the Shorn, turned into a war camp. It fills up as the player recruits the regions: Delver sappers, Fieldwarden snail-carts, Wildling archers (or Hedgewright axes), Pipewrights at the Valve pipes, Keep soldiers, and the Ant Thousand if you made peace. Services: **Shrine**, **Smith**, **Herbalist**, **General**, **Robin flight**, **War Table** (shows which allies have joined).

| Person | Role | Want | Secret / wound | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Knight-Commander Rowan Ironsod** | Commands the front | To end the hiding | – | "This time I don't get under the cart." |
| **Queen Marigold** (arrives in chapter 7) | Leads the final muster | To see her kingdom before it's mown | – | "So this is what outside smells like. Stubble and fear." |
| **Defector Brother Sedge** | Ex-Even Cut | Forgiveness | If the player spared him in "No Hats", he defects and gives you the Level's layout. | "I wanted an even lawn. I didn't want an empty one." |

### Sub-areas
- **The Shorn** (25-27): Even Cut patrols, clippers (zealots with shears), hand-mower acolytes.
- **The Level** (27-28, **dungeon/fort**): Even Cut stronghold, perfectly square. Boss: **High Edger Sedgewick**.
- **The Fence line** (28-29): things that fled the Shed: rust-rats, oil-slugs (black, slick) and grass-bag moths.
- **The Shed** (30): the final battle. See [quests.md](quests.md) chapter 8.
