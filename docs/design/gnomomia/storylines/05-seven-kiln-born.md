# The Seven Kiln-born

**Zone:** Birdbath Keep (hub), with claims in Mossbottom, Thatchford, Cloverfield, Hedgewood and the Rockery · **Levels:** 6-24 · **Quests:** 7 · **Leans toward:** none
**Unlocks after:** Three Accounts (Ch 3, main) · **Ties to main story:** Gnorwin Clayfoot is the first king and the one who threw the Stopping Stone. The claims set up Ch 6's Kiln Warden beat without answering it.

## Pitch
Archivist Basil Inkcap wants the seven Kiln-born checked. The songs name Gnorwin Clayfoot and disagree about the other six, and every village claims one. The player collects a claim from five villages (a carving, a verse and a relic each) and brings them back to the Archive, where the claims contradict each other and one name has been scratched out. The reward is a bead with nothing written on it, and the choice of what to write down. Nobody gets an answer.

## Roots in the three layers
- **Planting (3,000 years):** "The Long Lawn" says seven gnomes came out of the Kiln first, and gives Gnorwin the first place. Its other verses give five, six or eight, and every village's verse names a different gnome last out.
- **Charters (300 years):** The Archive holds three copies of the founding roll, the Seven Roll. Two list seven names. The third has a line scratched out with the seventh name cut through. Each charter guild claimed the Kiln-born from its own region, which is why Cloverfield, the Hedgewood and the Rockery all have a claim.
- **Rumbling (30 years):** After the First Rumble a crack opened in the Shard Mine's old face and showed a gnome-shaped mould in the rock. Old Lichen still sings the Hedgewood verse every winter, and says the blank line is the only part he is sure of.

## Key scenes and dialogue
1. **The Archive, quest 1.** Basil lays out three copies of the roll. "There are three accounts of that. All wrong in interesting ways. Seven names, and one of the copies has a hole in it. Go and ask the villages what they remember of their own."
2. **The well, quest 2.** Old Thistle, sweeping: "Moss? She sat on the rim and sang while the mushrooms came up. Sang it wrong, mind. Sang it like she was counting."
3. **The ford, quest 3.** Rye Grindwell, tapping a tally: "Rush carried the ford stones on his back, the song says. Count them. I did. There are five. Say it was seven gnomes, and I'll count you out of the river."
4. **The granary, quest 4.** Ma Haybale: "Vetch planted the first clover. Seven rows, the rhyme says. My granny sang six rows and no Vetch at all. You pick."
5. **The Shard Mine, quest 6.** Grout, with a hand on the rock: "It breathes out, that mould. Only when the Mower's further along. Thump. Turn. The mould's got a trowel beside its hand, if you were wondering. I wasn't."
6. **The Archive, quest 7.** Basil, five claims pinned on the table: "Five villages, five verses, one blot. Gnorwin is the only name nobody argues with. Which do you want written down?"

- **Birdbath Keep:** the Seven Ledger shelf in the Archive.

## The sad note
Old Lichen's daughter, Thyme, was taking the Ivy verse to the Sawdust Mill children about twenty-two years ago when a falling trunk came down the cutting line (the same fall that shattered the Wildling hunter Teasel Mulchwell; see storyline 09). Lichen still sings the verse every winter, and he still leaves the blank line empty. He says it is the only part of the song that has never changed.

## New canon
- Seven Kiln-born: Gnorwin Clayfoot (first king), Moss Kiln-Daughter (existing), Rush Stoutpot, Vetch Spadewell, Ivy Pailwright, Stonecrop Mallet, and a seventh whose name is scratched out.
- The Archive holds three copies of the founding roll, the Seven Roll. The third copy has the seventh name scratched out.
- Mossbottom's claim: Moss was last out of the Kiln and sang at the well while the mushrooms came up.
- Thatchford's claim: Rush carried the ford stones alone. Rye Grindwell counts five stones, not seven.
- Cloverfield's claim: Vetch planted the first clover in seven rows. Ma Haybale's grandmother sang six.
- Hedgewood's claim: Ivy stayed inside a hollow stump to wait for the Gardeners. Ivy's Hollow is a new sub-area behind Barkhollow.
- The Rockery's claim: Stonecrop's Mould is a gnome-shaped mould in the Shard Mine's old face, exposed by a crack after the First Rumble. A trowel is pressed beside its hand.
- Old Lichen's daughter Thyme died in a falling-trunk accident at the Sawdust Mill cutting line about twenty-two years ago, in the same fall that shattered Teasel Mulchwell (storyline 09).
- The Blank Name Bead is a Blooming trinket, ilvl 18.

## Lore checklist
1. Garden objects at gnome scale: the well ring, the Ford Stone, the granary measure, a stump, a mould in rock, a seed dibber, a tankard. Gnome names: yes.
2. Two time layers: Planting (the seven verses) and Charters (the Seven Roll), plus Rumbling (the crack, Lichen's verse). Yes.
3. Naming: the new Kiln-born are plant first names with soil, tool or household surnames (Stoutpot, Spadewell, Pailwright, Mallet). No puns.
4. Creatures with wants: none new.
5. Leans toward: none. The claims cut across all three ideas, which is the point.
6. Loose threads: nothing answered. The mould's trowel is a hint, the seventh name stays blank, and nobody says who the player is.
