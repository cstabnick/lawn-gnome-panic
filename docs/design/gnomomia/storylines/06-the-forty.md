# The Forty

**Zone:** Drainpipe Deeps, with Thatchford Downs, Hedgewood and Pedestal Ward · **Levels:** 12-25 · **Quests:** 5 · **Leans toward:** Tend it
**Unlocks after:** Chapter 3, *Into the Gutters* (quest 2 needs Chapter 5, *The Valve Maps*; quest 4 needs Chapter 6, *Down the Drains*) · **Ties to main story:** The Backflow is the Chapter 6 wing, and the forty trapped there are why Ida wants the Great Valve open.

## Pitch
Dill Puddleby has laid forty places at the Tipsy Toadstool for thirty-one years, and nobody has ever asked what the forty were carrying. The player does, and over five quests finds the tools and last words of several of them, meets the one who was hidden in the Hedgewood, lets the drowned echoes finish their shifts, and helps raise a stone at the top of the Deeps stairs with every name cut into it. It is worth doing because it is the one job in Fescue where the reward is being remembered properly.

## Roots in the three layers
- **Planting (3,000 years):** The Tall Ones' buried pipes under the Keep are the only water in Fescue that still runs where it was put. The harvest song *The Long Lawn* ends with a line gnomes take to mean *what is set down must be kept*.
- **Charters (300 years):** The Guild of Pipewrights dug the Deeps during the Dry Summer and kept a roll of every Pipewright who worked the waterworks. The roll is the origin of the forty.
- **Rumbling (30 years):** The Backflow drowned the lower Deeps and trapped forty Pipewrights. Ida's father stayed at the Valve to close it. Everyone else remembers the night differently.

## Key scenes and dialogue
1. **The Tipsy Toadstool, the table.** Dill: "Forty plates, every night. Somebody should at least know what they carried."
2. **The Gutters grate.** Madder's line is scratched into the iron, short and plain: *"WATER AT THE KNEE. CAULK HOLDING. TELL MAM."* Her tag is caught in the grate beside it.
3. **The Cistern barracks.** Ida, on the quest 2 return: "The maps have a W.P. in the corner. Woad never signed anything else. Keep his tag. He'd have wanted it used."
4. **Burnet's grave in the hedge.** Lichen: "Burnet said a name cut in bark is a fence, and he'd had enough of fences. So you choose. Bark or stone."
5. **Backflow, the fourth wheel.** A drowned Pipewright echo keeps turning the valve, *"Sticking. Sticking. Hold it."* When the wheel is set right it sighs, *"Done, then,"* and sits down in the silt.
6. **The Forty Stone.** Dill, with the snail-cart dust still on him: "Thistle sent word. Primrose goes in the middle, where the sun reaches first." Ida, looking up at the top name: "My father above them. Where the water can't reach."

## The sad note
Dill sets forty plates every night, and the stone will carry forty names. Not all of them can be told apart. Some of the forty were never found, and for them the stone has a name and nothing else. Madder's line is the last thing she wrote, and it asks for her mother, who is also dead. Nobody in this storyline tries to make that better. The stone is what the town can do, and it does it.

## New canon
- The Guild of Pipewrights kept a roll of every waterworks worker. The forty are the names on the Backflow roll.
- Speedwell Caulk, a seal-fitter, wrote the Letter from Puddlemere the day before the Backflow. The letter's first line reads, in full: *"The seal work isn't right, and I have written that down twice."* It names no one.
- Madder Wringer scratched her last line on the Gutters north grate and asked for word to go to her mother.
- Woad Plumbline, a surveyor, is one of the forty. His initials are in the margin of the Valve Maps.
- Burnet Tallow fled to the Hedgewood after the Backflow, was hidden by Old Lichen for nine years under another name, and died there. The Crown counts him among the forty.
- Master Pipewright Gromwell is carved above the forty on the Forty Stone, as the one who stayed at the Valve. He is not counted among the forty.
- Primrose Thistle's name is cut in the middle of the stone, at Old Thistle's request, sent through Dill.
- The Forty Stone stands in Pedestal Ward, at the top of the Deeps stairs.
- The Long Lawn, the harvest song, contains the line "what is set down must be kept."

## Lore checklist
1. **Garden object and gnome name?** Yes. Stamped tin tags, a caulk tin, a grate and a flagstone-sized memorial; the names are plain gnome names.
2. **Two time layers?** Yes. Charters (the Pipewright roll, the Deeps) and Rumbling (the Backflow, Lichen's hiding).
3. **Naming rule?** Yes. Wringer, Plumbline, Caulk and Tallow are tool or household surnames, with plant first names and no puns.
4. **Every pest wants something?** Yes. The drowned echoes want to finish their shift.
5. **Which idea does it lean toward?** Tend it. The stone, the tag, the swept path and the held table are all keeping.
6. **Did it give away a loose thread?** No. The letter says only that the seal work is wrong, and no one is named. The Puddlemere letter's line is the only seal hint, and quest 5 does not add to it.
