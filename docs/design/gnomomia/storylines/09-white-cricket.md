# The White Cricket

**Zone:** Hedgewood · **Levels:** 15-19 · **Quests:** 5 · **Leans toward:** Let it grow
**Unlocks after:** Side quest "Twitchett and Quickstring" (Ch 5, Bough) · **Ties to main story:** Barkhollow's hunters sit between the Wildlings and the Hedgewrights. The hunt does not change the Heartwood choice, but it gives the lodge's old code a real test.

## Pitch
Juniper Quickstring has a reply to Hazel Twitchett folded in her coat, and it has been there for two seasons. She will not send it until she has brought something home, and the only thing she will accept is the White Cricket, the pale singer the hunters have sung about longer than the Charter of Bough has existed. The hunt is a sling-and-leap fight, good for Slingers and fair to everyone else. It ends with a choice the lodge's own code should answer: let it sing, or take its wing.

## Roots in the three layers
- **Planting (3,000 years):** The old hedge verse, sung at Barkhollow's fire: *"When the Blade came up the Hedge, the little one sang, and the Blade turned round."* It disagrees with the songs about the Stopping Stone, and the lodge sings both.
- **Charters (300 years):** The Charter of Bough gives the Hedgewrights the game of the Hedgewood "wherever it is found." Two hundred and eighty years ago the Barkhollow hunters carved their own rule into the lodge's main beam: *Take what's hungry. Leave the rest to sing.* The Hedgewrights laughed at it and went on hunting.
- **Rumbling (30 years):** Juniper, Hazel and Teasel Mulchwell. Hazel left the lodge with a Wildling, Teasel, about 25 years ago. Teasel was shattered under a felled trunk on the Sawdust road. Hazel came home alone. Juniper stayed.

## Key scenes and dialogue
1. **The coat (Barkhollow, Juniper).** Juniper touches her collar and does not take anything out.
   - "It's in my coat. Two seasons. Don't look at me like that, I know how it sounds."
   - "A hunter doesn't walk into Thatchford empty-handed. It's the only rule I've kept."
2. **The Ear (Cricket Hedge).** The player listens through the acorn cup at the root.
   - Juniper: "Hear the crickets? Now listen under them. There's one that doesn't stop."
3. **The tenth (after the hunt's second half).** Hush drops to a tenth of its health and folds its wings. The song stops.
   - Juniper: "Hunters don't finish a beast that's stopped. Choose, and then we both live with it."
4. **The guildhall (Thatchford, Hazel).** Her line depends on the flag.
   - *Spared:* "She says you let it sing. Then you're the first hunter that lodge has ever sent me, and I'm not sure I forgive you for it."
   - *Taken:* "She says you took its wing. Then she'll wear it, and she'll never look at me while she does."
5. **Two pence (the same guildhall).** Hazel reads the reply, which is one line long: *"Come up the Hedge in spring. I'll have the kettle on."*
   - Hazel: "Two pence? You could have walked it over, Juniper." Then, to the player: "I'm not asking her to come home. I'm asking her to answer."

### Places
- **Cricket Hedge** (north-east of Barkhollow, between the lodge and the Privet's outer hedge; enemies 15-18). A clover-thick floor where the crickets sing. Holds the **Hunters' Ear**: an old acorn cup set in a root (interact, once).
- **Cricket-marks** on the outer hedge wall of the Elder Privet (interact, 3 of them). One carved notch per night the hunters heard Hush sing. Newest notches are the lodge's own.
- **The Code Beam** inside Barkhollow's lodge (optional examine, interact once). Carries the 280-year-old rule.

## The sad note
Teasel Mulchwell was not a Hedgewright and not a soldier. She was shattered under a felled trunk on the Sawdust road, twenty-two years ago, in a fall that came without a warning call. Hazel was on the far side of the road. She came home a year later and has never asked Juniper to forgive her for leaving, and Juniper has never told her there was anything to forgive. The reply in the coat is the only thing either of them has said about it.

## New canon
- Juniper has written Hazel every season for twenty-some years and never sent a reply. The reply in her coat is the only one she finished.
- Hazel left Barkhollow with Teasel Mulchwell, a Wildling hunter, about 25 years ago. Teasel was shattered under a felled trunk on the Sawdust road about 22 years ago. Hazel came home alone the following year.
- Juniper has been Huntmaster for twelve years.
- The Barkhollow hunters carved "Take what's hungry. Leave the rest to sing." into the lodge's main beam about 280 years ago, in answer to the Charter of Bough.
- A Planting verse says a little singing thing at the hedge turned the Mower round. The Archive does not record it. The lodge still sings it.
- Hush is a White Cricket, a mini-boss at level 17, that sings over the Elder Privet's outer hedge at night. Its song Slows, and it sets the other crickets quiet.
- Cricket Hedge, the Hunters' Ear, the cricket-marks and the Code Beam, as listed under New content.
- The Hedgewood epilogue gains one beat chosen by `cricket`: spared, crickets sing under the Privet every night; taken, the hedge is quiet and the Hedgewrights cheer, but the hunters do not.
- The Whitesong Jerkin, Hunter's Ear Horn and White Wing are the only new items in this storyline.

## Lore checklist
1. **Garden object at gnome scale, gnome name?** Yes. A real cricket, named "Hush" by the lodge, which is what a hunter would call a bird-quiet singer.
2. **Two layers of roots?** Yes: the Planting verse, the Charter of Bough's game clause and the carved beam, and the Rumbling story of Juniper, Hazel and Teasel.
3. **Naming rule, one pun at most?** Yes. "Hush", "Teasel Mulchwell", "Cricket Hedge", "Hunter's Ear". No puns.
4. **What does the creature want?** To sing its one song to the Privet every night, and be left alone.
5. **Which idea does it lean toward?** Let it grow. Sparing the cricket is the Wildling answer, and taking its wing is the Hedgewright answer, both framed through the hunters' code.
6. **Did it give away a loose thread?** No. Nothing here answers who the player is, who lit the Kiln, or what is beyond the Fence. The Planting verse is a myth that contradicts the Stopping Stone story, which is what the layers are meant to do.
