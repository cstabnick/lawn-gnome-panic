# Clippings

**Zone:** Hedgewood (Clippings) · **Levels:** 16-20 · **Quests:** 6 · **Leans toward:** Tend it
**Unlocks after:** Chapter 5, "The Elder Privet" (talk Splitwell) · **Ties to main story:** Chapter 5's Heartwood choice sets the branch for Quest 5, and Quest 6 feeds one epilogue beat at the Battle of the Verge.

## Pitch
The Hedgewright camp at Clippings cuts the Crown's timber under a 300-year charter, and it has paid for every log in people. The player works the camp through six quests: a height post with a child's notches, a log-run accident, a tithe that counts the living hedge as nothing, and a widow who needs the truth about her husband's killers. Chapter 5's Heartwood choice then decides how the crew cuts, and the storyline ends in one of two epilogues.

## Roots in the three layers
- **Planting (3,000 years):** The Mower stalled in the hedges and never cut them. The Hedgewood is the only wood in Fescue the Turning Blade did not touch. The songs disagree on what that means. Alder's crew says the Gardeners meant the hedge for a gnome's hand.
- **Charters (300 years):** The Charter of Bough gave the Hedgewrights the right to log the Hedgewood in return for timber, game and bowstring, "where the Wildlings had just settled." The camp's heaps of cut branches are its tithe-pile, and they gave Clippings its name. The Charter says "keep the Bough." The crew reads it as "cut the dead, keep the living."
- **Rumbling (30 years):** After the King's Fall the Crown let the Hedgewrights log twice as hard, and Wildling raids on the camps began. Reed Panwell died on the Sawdust Mill road three springs ago. Mallow Trowell was shattered under the log-run two summers ago.

## Key scenes and dialogue
1. **The long table.** Betony lays one plate more than there are crew.
   > "Eleven at this table. There were fourteen when I started cooking for it."
   > "Set one more plate and I won't ask who for."
2. **The height post.** Notches climb the post by the sawpit, one for each birthday. The top one is Fen's, at nine. Above it there is only bark.
   > "Trees grow back. Gnomes don't."
3. **The chock.** Rue gives the player Mallow's chock.
   > "I called it. He heard me. He held the rope anyway. That's the trouble with the good ones."
4. **Bareheaded.** Juniper's hunters found the raiders' trail on the Mill road. The raiders had no hats.
   > "Feral lads, not Wildlings. Burdock threw them out herself. Nobody asked her which side she'd rather they blamed."
5. **The Charter, read properly.** Alder explains the Charter to the player after Quest 5.
   > "It says keep the Bough. It doesn't say cut the lot. Take the dead wood, leave the living, and the hedge pays you back for three hundred years."
6. **The tithe.** Oakum settles the count at the scale.
   > "I signed it. I count what I signed. Count it with me, if you like. It comes out the same."

### Epilogue: hedge=peace
The Privet stands. The crew cuts only dead wood now, under Burdock's ribbons, and the camp calls it the Dead Shift. The clippings go back to the hedge roots instead of the tithe-pile, and Oakum files a tithe that counts living wood at nothing. The Charter is read aloud at the Clippings gate with a Wildling standing beside the reader. Alder works the sawpit himself and doesn't say much. Betony sets the plate. Fen's wren, if `fen=word`, sits on the top notch.

### Epilogue: hedge=felled
The Privet is a stump with new privet sprouting around its base. The Wildlings tend the new shoots, and the Hedgewrights don't look at them. The tithe is paid early, and the clippings heap grows to the height of the camp wall. Burdock's Tangle stays hostile, and Juniper's hunters have shut Barkhollow's south door. Alder sits on the stump at dusk with the bucksaw across his knees. Oakum counts the tithe twice. If `dock=ferals`, Betony tells the crew who raided the Mill road, and nobody argues. If `fen=word`, the carved wren on the post faces away from the camp.

## The sad note
Every notch on the height post is a child who grew up at Clippings, and all of them still work in the camp except the top one. Betony lays one plate more than the crew, and nobody asks who it is for. The camp's dead went down the log-run and along the Mill road in the same few years, and nobody sang for either one. Alder cut the wood he was paid to cut, and his son walked off into the hedge, and neither of them is wrong about the other. This note sits beside the Hedgewood's bark-names note and does not replace it.

## Lore checklist
1. **Garden scale and naming:** Yes. Sawpit, log-run, height post, tithe scale, the call "Bough!"; names Splitwell, Trowell, Spokeshave, Panwell, Oakum follow rule 5.
2. **Time layers:** Charters and Rumbling, with a Planting root.
3. **Naming:** Yes. Clippings is existing. One pun at most: none.
4. **What the pests want:** Web spiders want the food in their webs. Wildling scouts want the loggers gone. Reed's raiders are Ferals, whose want is the bestiary's "everything you own."
5. **Idea:** Tend it. Cut the dead, keep the living, and feed the ground with the clippings.
6. **Loose threads:** None answered. The Ferals were already established in Chapter 5's Mill Raid, and this storyline adds no fact about the Tall Ones, the Kiln, the player or beyond the Fence.
