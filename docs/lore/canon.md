# Core canon

What is true about gnomes in **every** game here, unless the game's setting lists a departure in
[settings.md](settings.md). It is deliberately short: the core is what a new game can lean on without reading
anything else. Deep lore belongs to a setting (Fescue's is in [docs/design/gnomomia/](../design/gnomomia/README.md)).

Each fact has a fact-sheet in `entries/core/` with its sources. *(draft)* marks a fact proposed here but not yet
leaned on by a game; change those freely.

## The facts

1. **The world is a garden, seen from gnome height.** Every landmark is an ordinary yard object at giant scale: a
   birdbath is a castle, a hose is a river, a rock garden is a mountain range. The player should recognise the
   object; the gnomes call it something else. → [the-garden](entries/core/the-garden.md)
2. **Gnomes are fired clay.** They are shaped and fired, not born. They don't bleed: they chip, crack and, at worst,
   shatter. Cracks are glued and glazed. Earthenware or porcelain depends on the setting. → [gnomes](entries/core/gnomes.md)
3. **Nobody ever sees the Tall Ones.** The giants who made the gardens (and the gnomes) are never shown or heard. Their
   traces are huge: footprints, dropped tools, whole houses. No human appears on screen, in any game.
   → [tall-ones](entries/core/tall-ones.md)
4. **Mowers are the great machine of gnome life.** Every setting has them, as the thing gnomes fear most or the thing
   they have learned to ride, or both. → [mowers](entries/core/mowers.md)
5. **Acorns are treasure.** Gnomes collect them, throw them, shoot them and count their wealth in them; a golden acorn
   is the prize of prizes. → [acorns](entries/core/acorns.md)
6. **A gnome's hat is who they are.** Teams are hat colours (Red Caps, Blue Caps; online, the player's colour goes on
   the hat). A gnome without a hat is hiding something. → [gnome-hats](entries/core/gnome-hats.md)
7. *(draft)* **The player's gnome is one gnome.** The gnome made in the Gnome Creator is the same gnome in every game
   and setting. How one gnome turns up in all of them is a loose thread, never answered.
   → [the-players-gnome](entries/core/the-players-gnome.md)
8. *(draft)* **Goblins are unfired peat-and-root folk.** Where gnomes are fired clay, goblins are packed loam, peat,
   bark, and river-grit cured by sun or compost-warmth. They never bleed: they crumble, shed peat-dust, or leak sap,
   and mend with damp loam and twine. → [goblins](entries/core/goblins.md)
9. *(draft)* **Gnoblins are the joint-offspring of gnomes and goblins.** Moulded together by a gnome-and-goblin
   household from clay and living root-loam and cured in a warm compost-bed, they rarely shatter and rarely bog down.
   → [gnoblins](entries/core/gnoblins.md)

## House style

How gnome lore is written in every setting. Fescue's [lore-rules.md](../design/gnomomia/lore-rules.md) is the
long, strict version; a setting may be stricter but not looser, apart from the departures it lists.

- **Names.** Gnome first names are plants (Fernwick, Tansy, Bramble); surnames are soil, tools and household things
  (Clayfoot, Potsworth, Stoutpot). Places join two plain garden words (Mossbottom, Thatchford). At most one pun per
  name. The Yard is allowed sillier nicknames (Big Lou, Granny Gnertrude); apocrypha anything.
- **Renamed objects.** Gnomes rename yard things in their own words (petrol is Black Dew, a marble is a Tall-eye).
- **Every pest wants something.** Garden creatures are hungry, scared or defending a nest, never evil for no reason.
- **No gore.** Things crack, scatter, flee and get mowed. Nothing bleeds red; a "bleed" is sap or clay dust. Holds even
  in horror (the Tall House).
- **Dry jokes, no pop culture.** Understated humour, no fourth-wall jokes, no references to real games or films
  (apocrypha excepted: a parody is the point).
- **Leave threads loose.** Some things stay unexplained (where the Tall Ones went, what is beyond the Fence, who the
  player is). Write hints, never answers, and list the thread in the entry so nobody answers it by accident.
- **Magic, where a setting has it, is what a garden needs:** sun, rain and growth. No demons or necromancy.
  *(draft for settings other than Fescue)*
