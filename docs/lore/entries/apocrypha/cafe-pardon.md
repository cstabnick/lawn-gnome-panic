The walk-in café on Teacup Row where Hector Disdain waits and never takes an order.

## What gnomes say

"Café Pardon has a very good menu. You may admire it. You may not order from it."

"The coffee arrives when it is ready. It was ready on Tuesday."

## The truth

The café is named for the first word everybody says in it. A player can walk in and sit down; whether anything comes of that is up to Hector's mood, which is the same every day. The gramophone on the terrace table plays Radio Adagio to the whole lane.

## In the games

Teacup Row, near x -1525, z 143 (the terrace gramophone). A walk-in interior: stand at a table and wait, and Hector will pass, sigh and not take your order.

Act IV (R-234, `m63-four-taps`): when Don Compost names his price, Mayor Barnaby "Two-Coat" Mulch is found not at his residence but at a terrace table of Café Pardon, where no HOA letter can be served past the Wickets (R-183). His second coat of beige is drying in the continental sun, and Hector Disdain has not taken his order in three days. The player collects him from the terrace and drives him from Pardonlin to the Mulch Club to sign. The café is the one place in the yard where the Mayor has been waited on, in the sense of being waited.

## Everyday

- **The menu (for reading only):** the card by the door lists nine dishes and no prices. A pencilled line at the foot reads "Please do not ask for the tenth." The pastry case is marked "Pastries: look, do not ask."
- **House rules:** "You may look at the menu. The menu may look at you. That is the service." Hector Disdain does not take orders; he takes note.
- **Notices:** a chalkboard reads "Today: coffee. Tomorrow: possibly coffee." A saucer by the window holds "one espresso, since Tuesday (12 saucers, 0 refills)". The lost-umbrella wall counts 212 umbrellas, claim tickets none, owners "sunny types". A queue by the wall reads "This queue formed by habit. Please join it."
- **Hours:** open when it opens. Closed for lunch, which is most of the day.
- **Sayings:** "A table at Café Pardon is a view, not a promise."
