Everything the Carburettors have left beside the Capwood Run: a pavilion clock that runs fast, a camp of spectators with opinions, a wall of tyres at the jump, a stump of lost hubcaps and a mower parked in a ford. None of it was planned; all of it is now tradition.

## What gnomes say

"The clock is five minutes fast. That is how the race starts early."
"Every hubcap on that stump was somebody's best day. And then their worst."
"The water is fine. It says so on the sign. The sign has never been in it."

## The truth

The Run's road has two named fords and one named jump, and every named thing on it has a story nobody will finish. The Gill Ford (a 30 m crossing, 0.35 m deep) and Spore Splash (28 m, 0.4 m) are Cap-water, which drags a mower and never stalls it (R-189). The Puffball Pop is the single jump, a 1.4 m kicker with a ramp 14 m up and 20 m down; six tyre stacks, the **Pop Tyre Wall**, stand round it and nobody remembers who stacked them. **The Fast Clock** is the start pavilion's clock, five minutes fast since the first race, under the banner "The Capwood Run: Start". **The Hairpin Camp** is two camp chairs, a cooler and a cardboard sign reading SEND IT, where Dorrit Verge and the other spectators arrive at dawn to have opinions about the line. **Lost at the Summit** is a hatchback with its bonnet up on the Fells summit, a flask and a folded map on its roof and a note that says LOST?; the driver is up there somewhere, looking at the view. **The Hubcap Stump** is a stump nailed round with every hubcap the Run has ever lost, in order of embarrassment. **The Parked Mower** sits in the shallows of the Gill Ford with its driver's boots drying on the seat, watched by the ford marshal Wader Pilch. Marshal Sprocket keeps the start line and says everyone is a Carburettor today.
