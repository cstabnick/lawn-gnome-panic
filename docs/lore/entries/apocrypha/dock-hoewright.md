The voice of 99.3 The Blade's days: loud, quick, and fond of the word "machine".

## What gnomes say

"One, two, Blade." Dock counts it on the hour and the whole of Turfton's dashboard counts it back. Gnomes who have
never met him have an opinion about his microphone.

## History

Born in a flowerpot on the Greenhouse Strip, the ninth of nine. His father kept Hoewright's Tools & Trowels, a shop
that sold a rake for every occasion and a hoe for none. Dock raced Zero-Turns in the ice trials on Frostbite Fells and
lost his last race by a whisker, when the bolt on his seat came loose on the final bend. He has never once let off the
throttle since, which is where the nickname comes from. (The nickname "Two-Stroke" belongs to his cousin Pip Potsworth,
so Dock was never going to use it.)

He started at Side-Valve Salvage, reading the garage's adverts for nothing between songs of his own choosing. Tansy
Clayfoot still has the first microphone on a hook by the door. He got a show of his own after playing a recording of
a Combine's engine over the air, in the middle of an HOA ban on "unlicensed noise", and was fined for it by the one
clerk who laughed. (That clerk is now on the other station. See Cress Caliper.)

## Quirks

- He will not say "mower" on air. Since the beige people complained, it has been "machine", and the HOA is "the beige
  people". He does not explain.
- Every show starts with the sound of a pull-cord, which he records himself in the garage.
- He reads the traffic in a voice that suggests he has seen it personally, and sometimes he has.

## In the games

- The Blade's banter (`radio/content.ts`): the sign-on, the machine speeches, the Side-Valve adverts, the Combine on the
  central lane, the grass-height advice for Reginald Vance.

## Loose threads

Why did the bolt come loose? Why does the static under his signal count backwards to one, on nights when he is not on
air? He has been asked and has said that the bolt was a sign, and that he is not sure of what.
