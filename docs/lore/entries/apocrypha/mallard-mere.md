The duck pond in the north-west of Turfton, a round mere of brown water with reeds at one side and a wooden duck house
on a raft. A gravel dog-walk loop goes all the way round it, and the dogs are never sure which way they are supposed to
go.

## What gnomes say

"Ducks don't own the Mere. They just have the best view of it." The mallards have been here longer than the street
plan, and the townsgnomes say they were the first to vote in City Hall elections, because they were the first to
ask for a pond.

## Inside & Stories

- The pond, eight metres across, with a stand of reeds on the far side and a few lily pads that the ducks count every
  morning.
- A duck house on a floating platform, with a ramp, a little pitched roof and a sign nobody can read from the bank.
- The dog-walk loop, a ribbon of gravel round the pond. Walkers keep to it. Dogs do not.
- Benches face the water from four sides, and lamps stand between the pond and the loop.
- Wildflowers along the south bank and a noticeboard on the north side, where the duck-feeding rules are pinned up
  under a lot of tiny drawings of ducks.

## In the games

Mallard Mere is the park at block centre (-30, 150). Its ducks are the park's own life: the game keeps a handful of
them on the water. Townsgnomes walk the dog loop and feed the ducks; players can sit on the benches.

## Loose threads

Whether the duck feeder is a townsgnome, a duck, or someone the ducks have trained.
