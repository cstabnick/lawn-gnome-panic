The Custom Shop's wheel counter: ten kinds of tyre, eleven kinds of rim, the steering wheels that go with them, and the camber and ride-height settings that decide how a mower sits on them.

## Tyres

- **Pudgy Puffers**: wide, round and squishy. Float over anything.
- **Skinny Sprinters**: thin as a ribbon. Built for show-offs and downhills.
- **Whitewall Wafers**: a neat white ring round each tyre. Very Sunday.
- **Mud-Grub Knobbies**: chunky tread, ready for any flowerbed.
- **Wagon-Spoke Hoops**: old wooden cart wheels with iron hoops. Bumpy and proud.
- **Flying Saucers**: flat, wide discs. Nobody can say how they grip.
- **Burr-Stud Spikes**: tread studded with burrs. Grips the lawn and the neighbour alike.
- **Balloon Bouncers**: huge soft tyres that bounce clean over kerbs and most of the way over a fence.
- **Cog Cleats**: gear-tooth tread that clicks along the path like a clock with an opinion.
- **Glow-Worm Hoops**: hoops with a glowing strip. They light the way, mostly.

## Rims

- **Five-Spoke Stars**: five clean spokes. A classic.
- **Chicken-Wire Mesh**: a fine criss-cross web. Looks busy even when parked.
- **Deep-Dish Buckets**: the rim dips in like a soup bowl.
- **Spinner Spinners**: blades that keep spinning after you stop. Distracting.
- **Flower-Power Rims**: petal-shaped spokes round a yellow centre.
- **Acorn-Cap Hubcaps**: a hubcap shaped like an acorn cap on every wheel.
- **Turbofan Rims**: slanted blades like a little jet engine.
- **Cog Rims**: rims cut as gears. They mesh with the road, sort of.
- **Bottle-Cap Rims**: crimped bottle-cap rims. Fizzy, and loud on potholes.
- **Sunflower Rims**: a big yellow sunflower on every wheel. The bees approve.
- **Saw-Blade Rims**: serrated, very sharp, and not to be hugged.

## Steering wheels

- **Racing Dish**: a small, flat-bottomed wheel with a leather rim.
- **Banjo Sprig**: three thin spokes and a wooden rim. Very old money.
- **Chunky Donut**: a fat, squashy rim. Gnomes can hold it with their whole arm.
- **Daisy Wheel**: a wheel with petals. It steers just the same.
- **Pretzel Wheel**: twisted into a knot. Nobody knows which way is up.
- **Ship's Helm**: a brass ship's wheel with spokes you can really spin. The mower has never been to sea.
- **Heart Wheel**: steers with love, and a slight wobble on the corners.
- **Square Wheel**: square, for a square mind. Corners are an adventure.
- **Sprocket Wheel**: toothy and chain-driven. Clicks every time you turn.

## Camber and ride height

Leaning the wheels is a courtesy among gnomes. Tops in is respect; tops out is a dare. Nobody in the shop will say
which one the customer is making.

"Slammed" and "lifted" are the two words for ride height. A mower is lowered by letting the air out of its cushion
springs, and lifted on a stack of acorn cups under each corner. Neither one is done in a hurry.

## What gnomes say

"Tops in, mate. Tops out is a dare."
