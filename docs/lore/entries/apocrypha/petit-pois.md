A pea-green city hatchback small enough to park in the gap between two other parked cars, sideways.

## Field Notes

The Petit Pois turns up in the traffic of the European yard, usually in a gap that nobody else would have tried. Its top speed is 24 units an hour, which is brisk for something that fits in a teacup saucer. It parks sideways because the gap is sideways, and it has never once been asked to explain itself.

Burl's Forecourt keeps a row of them out front, each one parked in a space that is technically a space. Burl says they are the easiest cars to sell and the hardest to get out of the lot. The doors open on the second attempt.

## What gnomes say

"I have parked it in the gap. The gap has parked me back. We are both content."
