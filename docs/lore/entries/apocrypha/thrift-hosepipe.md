The groundskeeper of Hedgeworth: patient, bent and wet to the knee, who talks to the grass, props the Kicked Board shut and hides moles under the ninth green.

Home: the groundskeeper's shed by the ninth green. Work: Hedgeworth grounds.

## What gnomes say

"Thrift says 'good morning' to the fairway. It is the only fairway in the garden that answers."

"Don't walk on the wet bits. He knows which bits."

## The truth

Thrift keeps the grass because the grass is alive, and he keeps the moles because they are why it is. He has sheltered a family under the ninth for three seasons and hopes Basil never asks.

## In the games

Built (batch 3, E15): watering the ninth green with his can and talking to it, between his shed door and the green. He is the giver of 48 (`m48-the-groundskeepers-secret`), chases the player off the wet fairway in 29 (`m29-the-kicked-board`), and is evacuated ahead of the Combine's line in 52 (`m52-the-test-cut`). He laid out holes 4 to 8 in the Bottom Garden by hand over three seasons (R-226), and the hosepipe that feeds the Drip (`the-drip`) still leaks: he is meaning to fix it.

## Everyday

- **Shops and stalls:** His shed by the ninth is not a shop, but it sells tea in chipped cups for what you feel like, and a pot of "Thrift's mixture" (grass seed, no questions) at two Acorns a handful.
- **Notices:** On the shed door: "Gone to see a man about a lawn." Inside, a row of watering cans from tiny to enormous, each named. The deck chair faces the ninth green and nothing else.
- **Sayings and habits:** "Good morning" to the fairway, "good night" to the green, nothing to the bunkers, which he does not trust. He pours a thimble of tea by the molehill at four each day and says it is for the grass.
