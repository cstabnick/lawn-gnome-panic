Gnoblins are the joint-offspring of a gnome and a goblin, moulded together from gnome-clay and living goblin-loam and cured slowly in a warm compost-bed. Half-ceramic and half-root, they rarely shatter like pure clay and rarely bog down like pure peat.

## How they are made

- When a gnome and a goblin keep a household together, they mix fine clay with packed peat-loam and living root-fibre, press it into a shared mould, and cure it in a **Warm Mound** (a compost-kiln warm enough to set the clay, too gentle to scorch the roots).
- The result is mottled terracotta-and-moss folk whose clay shell is laced with living rootlets. When chipped, the roots hold the pieces together until a glazer or peat-packer mends them.

## What gnomes and goblins say

- Gnoblins wear **patched caps**: half painted ceramic brim, half stitched dock-leaf or bark.
- In Fescue they often carry both a plant name and a root-or-soil byname (such as *Vetch Clayroot* or *Bramble-Grub Burlap-Toe*).

## Loose threads

Why a Warm Mound's gentle heat sets clay at all when no kiln fire touches it, and why gnoblins sometimes sprout tiny clover leaves behind their ears in spring.
