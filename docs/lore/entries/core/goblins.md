Goblins are small garden folk of packed peat, root-fibre, bark-knot, and river-grit—often tinted moss-green or peat-olive from pond-silt—unfired cousins to the clay gnomes. They never bleed: when hurt they crumble, shed peat-dust, or leak sap, and mend by packing fresh damp loam and binding with twine.

## What gnomes say

- In Fescue, gnomes call them **the Unfired** or **Loam-folk**, and say they rose in the damp dark under the Shed, behind the Compost Heap, and in the silt of the Drainpipe Deeps without ever seeing a kiln.
- In the Yard, goblins are noisy neighbours from behind the compost bin and under the porch steps who race snails and wheelbarrows, wager on toadstool pull-tabs, and bang out loud, head-bobbing rhythms on tin pot-lids and rusted bucket-rims—a clatter gnomes politely call **goblin metal**.

## What goblins say

- Goblins call gnomes **Glaze-skins** or **Kettle-baked**, admire their bright painted hats, and tease them for cracking over a dropped pebble.
- A dry summer stiffens a goblin's joints; too much rain makes them sprout weeds behind their ears. Neither is fatal, though both are considered undignified.
- When a dispute gets serious, older goblins swear their oaths on **the Goblinomicon**.

## Loose threads

Whether the Tall Ones shaped the first goblins from compost and forgot to fire them, or whether the garden itself packed them together after the Leaving.
