The way Gnorwin carried the Stopping Stone, from the Kiln in the Rockery through the Rockery pass to the barred door of the Shed on the Verge. It is the ride the game today calls "The Kiln & Shed".

## What gnomes say

- Planting: Gnorwin carried the Stone from the Kiln and threw it into the Blade's mouth; the Kiln-born then rolled the Mower back into the Shed and barred the door.
- Seven stones, the Rests, line the road, one for each Kiln-born. One carries the Clayfoot mark, a footprint in clay. The other six are scratched all over with names by gnomes of many villages (MOSS among them) until none is legible, because every village claims one.
- The King's cart still stands on the Shorn with the grass mown perfectly round it.

## The truth

It is a worn path between a stone hearth and a shed, with petrol on it. The Kiln mouth is cold: the glow on the road is the Kilnwarden's chest coals and the Glowmoths. The Mower's silhouette behind the door is the Shed's own shadow.

## In the games

The course called "The Kiln & Shed" today: a furnace arch, Stopping Stone obelisks, a Mower silhouette and a scorched Verge trail. Snap never shows the Mower move, the Kiln lit or the Stone fired.

## Loose threads

Who the other six were. The Kiln lit itself four nights before Gnomomia begins; Snap does not show that.
